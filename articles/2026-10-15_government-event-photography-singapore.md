# Government Event Photography in Singapore: GeBIZ, bizSAFE and PDPA Requirements

**Short answer:** Most Singapore government and statutory board events buy photography through **GeBIZ**, usually as a quotation, with work starting only after a Purchase Order is issued. On top of price and portfolio, agencies typically check five things:

- the right **safety certification** (bizSAFE Level 3 is a common requirement for on-site work)
- a plan for **personal data under the PDPA**
- crew who can meet **venue security** requirements
- clear terms on **who owns the photos**
- **invoicing** through the agency's system

This guide covers each one, so procurement officers and event teams know what to ask for, and what a vendor should already have in place.

*Written by Ricky Ee, Director of RS Photography. RS Photography is GeBIZ-registered and bizSAFE Level 3 certified, and has photographed public-sector events in Singapore since 2013.*

## How agencies buy event photography

### GeBIZ quotations and tenders

GeBIZ is the Singapore Government's e-procurement portal. Agencies publish their requirements there and vendors submit offers online. How the purchase is run depends on its value:

- **Small value purchases** for short, low-value jobs
- **Quotations (ITQ)** for most event photography and videography. The Ministry of Finance notes that more than 80% of government procurement opportunities are at quotation level, and that vendors do not need Government Registration Authority (GRA) registration to take part in quotations. [EXTERNAL LINK: MOF – Review of Government Electronic Business policy]
- **Tenders (ITT)** for large or long-running contracts

The value thresholds are set by the Ministry of Finance and are updated from time to time, so check the current rules on GeBIZ or with your procurement team.

### Period contracts

Agencies with many events a year, such as schools, institutes and community organisations, often run a **period (term) contract**. It fixes hourly or per-event rates for 12–24 months, so each event can be ordered without a new quotation. If your team runs more than a handful of events a year, ask procurement whether a period contract would save time.

### Schools and other platforms

Some schools and social service agencies collect quotations through other platforms, such as TenderBoard, instead of GeBIZ. The same documents are usually needed either way.

### The Purchase Order comes first

For government work, **the PO comes before the shoot**. A good vendor confirms the date, crew and scope in writing as soon as the quotation is awarded. If your PO will take time to process, say so, so the date can be held.

## bizSAFE: what level does a photographer need?

bizSAFE is the Workplace Safety and Health Council's programme for building workplace safety capability. It runs from Level 1 to Level 5, plus bizSAFE Star. [EXTERNAL LINK: WSH Council – bizSAFE programme]

There is no single whole-of-government rule. Each agency sets the level in its quotation or tender documents. **Level 3 is common** when vendors work on site, because it means the company has had its risk management assessed by an approved auditor. Many quotations also ask subcontractors on site to meet the same level.

For event photography, the risks sound small but are real:

- cables and light stands in walkways
- crew working on stage edges or raised platforms
- drones over crowds (only with a CAAS-licensed operator and venue approval)
- late teardown when venue staff are already clearing up

**RS Photography is bizSAFE Level 3 certified.** We include the certificate with every government quotation.

## PDPA: who is responsible for event photos?

Photos of identifiable people are personal data. This is the part of government event photography that is most often misunderstood.

### Agencies and vendors are covered differently

- **Public agencies** are excluded from the Personal Data Protection Act. They follow the government's own data management rules instead.
- **Vendors working for public agencies are not excluded.** Since **1 February 2021**, the PDPA exclusion covers only public agencies themselves. A photography company acting for an agency must still meet its own PDPA obligations, including protecting the photos and not keeping them longer than needed. [EXTERNAL LINK: PDPC decision on Iapps Pte Ltd, 2021]

In practice, both sides should agree on:

1. **Notice at the event.** Signage at registration and an announcement that photography is taking place.
2. **An opt-out.** For example, a coloured lanyard or sticker for guests who do not want to be photographed, briefed to every photographer.
3. **Minors.** Schools and youth events need parental consent arrangements set by the school or agency.
4. **Storage and transfer.** Photos delivered through a secure link rather than open public galleries, and removed from the vendor's systems after the retention period in the contract.
5. **Use in the vendor's portfolio.** Agreed in writing. Many agencies do not allow it, or only after approval.

## Security, protocol and the guest of honour

Government events often involve a minister, senior officials or other dignitaries. Plan for:

- **Crew registration.** Restricted venues such as SAF camps, the Istana or secure facilities usually need each crew member's name and identification details days in advance. Send these early, and don't swap crew at the last minute.
- **Restricted areas.** Some rooms, screens or equipment must not be photographed. The vendor should brief every photographer on these before the event.
- **GOH programme.** Arrival, walk-about, speech, presentation and group photo usually happen in a tight window. The photographer needs the run-of-show and the protocol officer's contact.
- **Dress code.** Usually dark office wear. Uniformed events may have specific rules.
- **Same-day photos.** Press releases and agency social media posts often need selects within hours. Ask for this in the quotation so it is priced in.

## Who owns the photos?

Government contracts commonly ask for the agency to **own the photos outright**, or for a licence wide enough to use them in any channel without asking again. Before you award, check:

- **Ownership or licence.** Does the quotation give the agency the rights its contract requires?
- **Raw files.** Are unedited files included, or only the edited selection?
- **Delivery format.** Agencies often need high-resolution files for print plus web-sized versions.
- **Credit lines.** Is a photo credit required or forbidden in publications?

Clear terms at quotation stage avoid disputes when the photos appear in an annual report two years later.

## Vendor checklist for procurement officers

Before you award, confirm the vendor can provide:

- [ ] Registration on GeBIZ (or the platform you are using)
- [ ] bizSAFE certificate at the level your quotation requires
- [ ] A written plan for PDPA notice, opt-out and data retention
- [ ] Names and IDs of the actual crew, if the venue needs them
- [ ] A portfolio of comparable public-sector events
- [ ] Photo ownership or licence terms that match your contract
- [ ] Turnaround times, including same-day selects if needed
- [ ] Nett pricing that matches the PO

### Is bizSAFE required for event photographers?
It depends on the agency's quotation or tender documents. There is no single government-wide rule, but bizSAFE Level 3 is a common requirement for vendors working on site. RS Photography is bizSAFE Level 3 certified.

### Does the PDPA apply to photos taken at government events?
