# Do You Need a Professional Event Photographer, or Will a Phone Do?

**Short answer:** Smartphones are good enough for **small, informal, well-lit internal events** and quick social media stories. A professional event photographer is worth it when **any** of these apply:

- the event is in **low light** or on a **stage** (dinners, conferences, ballrooms)
- **VIPs, speakers, guests of honour or clients** are present
- the photos will be used for **press, annual reports, websites or advertising**
- you need **every key moment** covered, while your staff are busy running the event
- you need **consistent, edited photos delivered on time**

Many organisations combine both: **a professional for the key moments**, and **staff phones for behind-the-scenes stories**.

## When a phone is enough

- **Small internal events**: a team lunch, a birthday or a short briefing
- **Good, even light**: daylight or a bright office
- **Social media stories** that disappear after 24 hours
- **Behind-the-scenes content**: setup, the team preparing, candid moments
- **No VIPs or external guests** who expect professional photos

For these, a staff member with a recent phone and a few simple tips (below) will do the job.

---

## When a professional photographer is worth it

| Situation | Why phones struggle |
|---|---|
| **Low light** (dinners, ballrooms, evening events) | Phone photos become blurry, grainy or orange; faces look flat |
| **Stages and speakers** | Speakers are far away and lit by spotlights; phones can't zoom far without losing quality |
| **Fast moments** (awards, handshakes, ribbon-cutting, lo hei) | The moment lasts seconds; phones and busy staff often miss it |
| **VIPs and guests of honour** | They expect a professional at the event, and the photos go to their offices too |
| **Press, annual reports and ads** | Need high resolution, correct colour and careful editing |
| **Large events** | One person can't cover registration, stage, booths and networking at the same time |
| **Branding** | Consistent style, colour and quality across the whole gallery |

## The hidden cost of "free" photos

Asking staff to take photos isn't really free:

- **Staff time**: the person taking photos isn't doing their real job at the event, often during the busiest moments.
- **Missed moments**: the organiser is the person most needed when something goes wrong, exactly when a key moment happens.
- **Editing and sorting**: hundreds of phone photos still need selecting, editing and organising.
- **Quality you can't fix later**: blur, bad light and closed eyes can't be repaired.
- **Reuse**: photos that aren't good enough for the website, press or next year's marketing mean you have nothing to reuse.

## The best of both: combine them

- **Professional coverage for the key moments**: arrivals, speeches, awards, the guest of honour, group photos.
- **Staff phones for stories**: setup, behind-the-scenes, quick posts during the event.
- **Same-day selects from the photographer** for LinkedIn, so staff don't have to post their own photos of the stage. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/same-day-event-photo-delivery-singapore "same-day event photo delivery"].
- **A short booking**: for a two-hour event, a short professional booking for the main programme may be all you need.

## If you use phones: simple tips

- **Clean the lens** with a soft cloth before you start.
- **Find the light**: face people towards windows or stage light; avoid shooting into bright windows.
- **Get closer instead of zooming**: digital zoom lowers quality.
- **Hold the phone steady** and take several shots of each moment.
- **Shoot horizontal** for websites and LinkedIn, vertical for stories.
- **Turn off the flash** for distant subjects; it only lights the people in front.
- **Ask before photographing guests**, and respect anyone who opts out.

## FAQ

### Do I need a professional photographer for a company event?
Not always. Phones are enough for small, informal, well-lit internal events and quick stories. A professional is worth it for low light, stages, VIPs and speakers, large events, and photos for press, reports, websites or ads.

### Why are phone photos of events often poor?
Most problems come from low light, distance and speed. Dinners and ballrooms are dark, speakers on stage are far away, and key moments last seconds. Phones produce blurry, grainy or orange photos in these conditions, and busy staff miss moments.

### Can staff take event photos instead of hiring a photographer?
For small internal events, yes. For larger events, the staff member taking photos can't do their real job, often misses key moments, and still has to sort and edit the photos afterwards.

### Can we use both a professional photographer and staff phones?
Yes, and many organisations do. The professional covers key moments such as speeches, awards and group photos, while staff capture behind-the-scenes stories for social media.

### Does the PDPA apply to photos taken by staff on phones?
Yes. The PDPA applies to photos of identifiable people whoever takes them. Tell guests about photography, offer an opt-out and handle the photos securely.
