# Community and Religious Celebration Photography in Singapore

**Short answer:** Anniversaries and celebrations for **religious, cultural and community organisations** combine three kinds of event:

- **a service or ceremony**, which needs respect and discretion
- **a formal programme**, with a guest of honour and speeches
- **a community gathering**, with families of all ages

Agree with the organisation's leaders **what may be photographed during worship or rituals**, follow **dress and conduct rules**, and plan the photos as an **archive** that members will look back on for decades.

*Written by Ricky Ee, Director of RS Photography. We photograph anniversaries, celebrations and community events for organisations of different faiths and cultures in Singapore.*

---

## Respecting worship and rituals

This is the most important part of the brief. Agree on it with the clergy, committee or organisers before the day.

- **What may be photographed.** Some services, prayers or rituals may not be photographed at all, or only from certain positions.
- **Flash.** Often not allowed during worship or sacred moments. Photographers use available light and quiet cameras.
- **Movement.** Stay in agreed positions during services, and move only between segments.
- **Sacred spaces and objects.** Some areas, such as altars or prayer halls, may be off-limits, or require shoes removed or heads covered.
- **Dress code.** Modest, dark clothing is usually expected. Follow the specific rules of the place of worship.
- **Conduct.** Photographers are guests: quiet, discreet and respectful at all times.

**When in doubt, ask.** A short walk-through with a committee member before the event avoids mistakes on the day.

## The formal programme

Anniversary celebrations often include a formal part, sometimes in a hotel ballroom or hall:

- **Guest of honour** arrival, welcome and speech
- **Leaders and founders**: past and present clergy, presidents or committee members
- **Anniversary moments**: cake cutting, unveiling a commemorative plaque or book, launching a fund or project
- **Recognition**: long-serving members, volunteers and donors
- **Cultural performances**: choirs, dances, music and traditional performances

## The community gathering

These photos are often the most treasured:

- **Families and generations** together: grandparents, parents and children
- **Long-time members** with each other
- **Volunteers** who made the event happen
- **Group photos** by ministry, group, committee or family

A **group photo spot** with a backdrop, and a photographer dedicated to families, works well for large celebrations.

---

## Cultural performances and traditions

Many celebrations in Singapore include traditional performances: lion and dragon dances, cultural dances, choirs, processions and music.

- **Learn the sequence** in advance, so the photographer is in place for the key moments.
- **Respect performers' space.** Don't block stage entrances or movement paths.
- **Capture the audience** too: reactions are part of the story.
- **Ask about rituals** within performances that should not be interrupted or photographed up close.

## Planning the archive

An anniversary is part of the organisation's history. Plan for:

- **Leaders and founders**: formal portraits of current and past leaders, while they are together
- **Historic group photos**: everyone present, from a raised position
- **The building and spaces**, as they are today
- **Commemorative book or video**: interviews and archive photos collected in the months before
- **Proper storage**: named, organised files, with copies held by the organisation

## Children, families and privacy

- **Tell members** that photography will take place, in announcements and on signage.
- **Offer an opt-out** for anyone who prefers not to be photographed.
- **Take extra care with children.** Use photos of children publicly only with parents' agreement.
- **Keep galleries private**, shared with members rather than posted publicly in full.

## FAQ

### Can a photographer take photos during a religious service?
Only as agreed with the clergy or organisers. Some services, prayers or rituals may not be photographed, flash is often not allowed, and photographers stay in agreed positions. A walk-through before the event helps clarify what is allowed.

### What should photographers wear at a place of worship?
Modest, dark clothing is usually expected, and some places require shoes to be removed or heads to be covered. Follow the specific rules of the place of worship.

### What should we photograph at a community anniversary?
The service or ceremony as permitted, the guest of honour and speeches, leaders and founders, anniversary moments such as a cake cutting or unveiling, cultural performances, volunteers, families across generations, and a historic group photo.

### How do we plan photos for a commemorative book or video?
Start several months before the anniversary: photograph leaders and the building, collect archive photos, and film interviews with long-time members. Then add the celebration itself and organise all files for the book or video.

### How should photos of children be handled at community events?
Tell members photography will take place, offer an opt-out, keep galleries private, and use children's photos publicly only with parents' agreement.
