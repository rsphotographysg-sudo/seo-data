# Annual Dinner and Gala Photography in Singapore: A Planning Guide

**Short answer:** For an annual dinner or gala in Singapore, plan for:

- **one photographer per 200–250 guests**
- coverage from **guest arrival to the last lucky draw**
- a **table-photo plan** so every table is photographed between courses
- a **photo booth** if you want guests to take something home

Budget about **S$600–S$1,200** for a small dinner with one photographer, and **S$2,000–S$3,500** for a large ballroom event with two photographers. A photo booth with instant prints starts at **S$800**. Book early: November to January is peak season for dinner-and-dance (D&D) events.

*Written by Ricky Ee, Director of RS Photography. We have photographed annual dinners, galas and award nights in Singapore since 2013.*

## What makes dinner photography different

An annual dinner is not a conference in evening wear. It has its own challenges:

- **Low light.** Hotel ballrooms are dimmed for atmosphere, with coloured uplights and a bright stage. Photographers need fast lenses and off-camera flash used discreetly.
- **Everyone wants a photo.** Staff expect to find themselves in the gallery, not just the CEO.
- **Moments are scattered.** Awards, performances, toasts and lucky draws are spread across three or four hours, with long dinner courses in between.
- **The photos are for staff.** The gallery is shared across the company, so good table photos and candid moments matter as much as stage shots.

## Annual dinner shot list

Use this as a starting point and add your own moments.

### Arrival
- [ ] Guests arriving and registration
- [ ] Step-and-repeat or themed backdrop photos, in small groups as people arrive
- [ ] Welcome drinks and early mingling

### Opening
- [ ] Wide shot of the decorated ballroom before guests sit (the only time it looks perfect)
- [ ] Emcee opening and the CEO's or chairman's welcome speech
- [ ] Toast, with the whole room raising glasses
- [ ] For Chinese New Year dinners: the **lo hei** toss at the main table and a few staff tables

### Programme
- [ ] Performances, with reactions from the audience
- [ ] Long-service and annual awards: handshake, award held up, and a group photo of all winners
- [ ] Best-dressed and theme contests
- [ ] Lucky draw: the name being drawn and the winner's reaction

### Tables and candids
- [ ] **One photo of every table**, all guests looking at the camera
- [ ] Candid conversations, laughter and the dance floor
- [ ] Department or team groups, if requested

### Closing
- [ ] Organising committee group photo
- [ ] Management team photo
- [ ] Final dance floor or closing moment

## How to get a photo of every table

This is the most common complaint after an annual dinner: "Our table wasn't photographed." It is easy to avoid with a plan.

1. **Share the table plan** with the photographer: table numbers and which department sits where.
2. **Shoot tables between courses,** when guests are seated but not eating. Avoid the minutes right after a dish is served.
3. **Ask the emcee to announce it** ("Our photographers will be coming round to every table"). Guests will wait for them instead of wandering off.
4. **Assign tables to each photographer** at large events, for example tables 1–30 and 31–60.
5. **Tick tables off** on a printed table plan as they go.

A photographer can usually shoot 25–35 tables an hour this way. For 60 tables, two photographers can cover every table within the first two courses.

## How many photographers do you need?

| Guests | Photographers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 150 | 1 | One photographer can cover the stage and every table |
| 150–300 | 1–2 | Two if you want backdrop photos during arrival and full table coverage |
| 300–600 | 2 | One for the stage programme, one for tables and candids |
| 600+ | 3 or more | Add one for the backdrop or VIP area |

Add a **photo booth** if you want guests to queue for fun photos without taking the photographers away from the programme.

---

## Photo booths: are they worth it?

A photo booth is popular at annual dinners because:

- guests take home a **printed photo** on the night
- it keeps guests entertained during long dinner courses
- it creates hundreds of happy, informal photos for internal comms
- props and frames can match the dinner theme

Booths start from **S$800**. When you book one, check:

- the hours the booth runs
- whether prints are unlimited
- whether the print frame can carry your logo and the event name
- whether digital copies are included in the gallery

## Timing: book early for D&D season

In Singapore, annual dinners cluster in **November to January**. Chinese New Year dinners follow in **January and February**. Popular Friday and Saturday dates go first.

- **Book 6–8 weeks ahead** for November–January Fridays and Saturdays.
- **Send the run-of-show one week before**, including award order and lucky-draw timing.
- **Confirm the theme**, so the photographers can dress to fit in (dark formal wear is the safe default).

## Getting photos to staff quickly

The gallery is often the most-shared thing after an annual dinner. Make it easy:

- **Same-night highlights** for the CEO's thank-you email or the company's social media. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/same-day-event-photo-delivery-singapore "how same-day delivery works"].
- **A private online gallery** organised by programme segment and table number, so staff can find their own table fast.
- **Downloads in web size** for staff, and full resolution for the comms team.
- **A gallery link that expires** after an agreed period, for privacy.

### Privacy at staff events

Staff photos are still personal data. Tell staff that photography is taking place, offer an opt-out, and keep the gallery private rather than public. See our [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/pdpa-event-photography-consent-guide "PDPA event photography consent guide"].

A good photographer also curates. Unflattering mid-bite shots and moments people would rather forget should not reach the company gallery.
