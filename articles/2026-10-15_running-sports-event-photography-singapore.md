# Running and Sports Event Photography in Singapore: A Planning Guide

**Short answer:** For a run, race or sports event, plan photography around **fixed photo points** along the route and the key moments:

- the **start**
- the **finish line**
- the **podium**
- **sponsor areas**

Assign at least one photographer to each point. Agree how participants will **find their own photos**, for example a gallery searchable by **bib number**. Night races need photographers experienced with **flash and fast-moving subjects in low light**.

*Written by Ricky Ee, Director of RS Photography. We photograph runs, corporate sports events and community sports days in Singapore.*

---

## What a race or sports event needs

| Area | What to capture |
|---|---|
| **Race village / event hub** | Registration, bag drop, sponsor booths, warm-up, crowds |
| **Start** | Flag-off by the guest of honour, wide shot of the start pen, runners crossing the line |
| **Along the route** | Runners at landmarks, water points, cheering zones |
| **Finish line** | Every finisher crossing, emotions, medal presentation |
| **Podium and prizes** | Winners in each category with sponsors and the guest of honour |
| **Sponsors** | Logos on arches, banners and bibs; sponsor representatives with winners |
| **After-party / activities** | Stage programme, lucky draws, community moments |

---

## Planning photo points

Photographers can't follow every runner, so plan **fixed points** where many runners pass.

- **Start**: one photographer on a raised position for wide shots, and one at ground level for the flag-off.
- **Landmarks**: one or two points along the route with a recognisable Singapore backdrop. These are the photos runners share most.
- **Final stretch**: a photographer 50–100 m before the finish, for runners in full stride.
- **Finish line**: a photographer facing the finish arch, for every finisher crossing.
- **Medal and recovery area**: candid shots of finishers with medals.

**Ask the organiser for the route map early,** and agree safe positions with the race director and marshals. Photographers must never block runners or emergency access.

## How many photographers?

| Participants | Photographers | Typical coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 500 | 2–3 | Start, finish, and race village |
| 500–3,000 | 3–6 | Start, 1–2 route points, finish, race village, podium |
| 3,000+ | 6 or more | Several route points, multiple finish angles, dedicated sponsor and VIP coverage |

Long routes and multiple categories with different start times need more photographers, or photographers who move between points.

## Night runs and low light

Night races are popular in Singapore but hard to photograph:

- **Use flash** at fixed points to freeze runners and show faces clearly.
- **Choose lit areas** for photo points, such as landmarks, the finish arch and lit sections of the route.
- **Capture the atmosphere**: light installations, crowds and the skyline at the start and finish.
- **Plan for safety**: photographers wear reflective vests and stay in marshalled positions.

---

## Participant galleries: helping runners find their photos

Participants want **their own photo**, quickly. Options include:

- **Bib-number search**: photos tagged by bib number, so runners type their number to find their photos.
- **Galleries by location and time**: for example "Finish line 7:00–7:30 am", so runners can find themselves near their finish time.
- **Fast delivery**: highlights on social media the same day, and the full participant gallery within a few days.

Agree in advance how photos will be tagged and published, and whether participants can download them free or the organiser offers them as part of registration.

Tell participants in the race terms and at registration that photography takes place and how galleries will be published. Offer a way to request removal. See our [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/pdpa-event-photography-consent-guide "PDPA event photography consent guide"].

## Sponsors and branding

Race sponsors want visibility:

- **Logos on arches, backdrops and bibs** visible in key photos
- **Sponsor representatives** at the flag-off and the podium
- **Sponsor booths** in the race village with participants
- **A sponsor folder** in the gallery for quick delivery after the event

## Corporate sports days

Company sports days and inter-department games use the same approach, on a smaller scale:

- photograph **every team** and every event
- capture **cheering and team spirit**, not only winners
- plan a **group photo** for each department
- deliver quickly for internal comms

## FAQ

### How many photographers does a running event need?
Usually two to three for up to 500 participants, three to six for 500 to 3,000, and six or more for larger races, covering the start, one or more route points, the finish, the race village and the podium.

### Where should photographers be placed along a race route?
At the start, at one or two recognisable landmarks, 50 to 100 metres before the finish, facing the finish arch, and in the medal area. Positions should be agreed with the race director and marshals, so photographers never block runners or emergency access.

### How do runners find their photos after a race?
Through a gallery searchable by bib number, or galleries organised by location and time. Highlights can be posted the same day and the full participant gallery within a few days.

### How are night runs photographed?
With flash at fixed photo points in lit areas, such as the finish arch and landmarks, plus wider atmosphere shots of lights, crowds and the skyline. Photographers wear reflective vests and stay in marshalled positions.

### What should sponsors get from race photography?
Photos with their logos on arches, backdrops and bibs, their representatives at the flag-off and podium, their booths with participants, and a sponsor folder for quick delivery.
