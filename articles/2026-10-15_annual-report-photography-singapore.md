# Annual Report Photography in Singapore: A Planning Guide

**Short answer:** Annual report photography usually covers four things:

- **board and leadership portraits**
- **operations**: where and how the business works
- **people**: staff, customers and community
- **a cover image**

Plan it as **one or two shoot days** booked **6–10 weeks before the design deadline**. Use a brief from the design team, so photos fit the layouts and the year's theme.

The same shoot can also build a **corporate image library** for the website, sustainability report and press kit.

*Written by Ricky Ee, Director of RS Photography. We photograph leadership portraits, operations and corporate image libraries for companies and organisations in Singapore.*

## What an annual report usually needs

| Section | Photos needed |
|---|---|
| **Cover** | One strong image that reflects the year's theme |
| **Chairman's and CEO's messages** | Portraits of the chairman and CEO, often in a business setting |
| **Board of directors** | Individual portraits, sometimes a group photo, in a consistent style |
| **Senior management** | Individual portraits matching the board's style |
| **Operations review** | Facilities, products, services and people at work |
| **Sustainability / ESG** | Staff, community programmes, environmental initiatives, safety |
| **Year in review** | Key events of the year: launches, openings, awards and partnerships |

Much of the "year in review" comes from events photographed during the year, so keep event galleries organised and easy to search.

---

## Timing: work back from the design deadline

Many companies with a **December financial year-end** work on their annual reports in the first months of the new year. A typical schedule:

1. **Brief and shot list**: 10–12 weeks before the design deadline
2. **Book board and management portraits**: they have the busiest calendars, so book first
3. **Shoot days**: 6–8 weeks before the design deadline
4. **Selection and retouching**: 1–2 weeks
5. **Delivery to the designer**: 4–6 weeks before the deadline

**Board portraits** are often easiest on the day of a **board meeting**, when all directors are in one place. Ask the company secretary for the date early.

---

## Board and leadership portraits

- **Keep one consistent style** for all directors and management: background, lighting, crop and pose.
- **Plan 10–15 minutes per person** for formal portraits, more if you also need an environmental portrait.
- **Set up next to the boardroom** on board meeting days, so directors can step out between agenda items.
- **Shoot extra versions**: horizontal and vertical crops, and a little extra space around the person for layout flexibility.
- **Re-use the setup.** Record lighting and camera settings so new directors next year match the existing portraits.

## Operations and people

These photos show the business behind the numbers.

- **Real people at real work**, not stock photos. Readers and investors notice the difference.
- **Get permission and brief staff** before shooting in offices, plants, warehouses, labs or retail outlets.
- **Follow site rules**: safety PPE in plants and construction sites, hygiene rules in food and healthcare settings, and restrictions on filming equipment or screens.
- **Show diversity and range**: different roles, teams and locations.
- **Shoot for the layout**: wide images for spreads, details for sidebars, and space for text over images.

## Sustainability and ESG reports

Sustainability reporting has become standard for many listed and large companies. Photos support each section:

- **Environment**: energy projects, recycling and green buildings
- **Social**: staff training, wellbeing, safety and community programmes
- **Governance**: board and leadership

**Authenticity matters most.** Use real initiatives and people, and avoid images that overstate what the company is doing.

## Building an image library

One annual report shoot can supply a year of corporate images:

- **website** banners and "About us" pages
- **press kits** with leadership portraits
- **recruitment** pages and LinkedIn
- **proposals and tenders**
- **sustainability report** and investor presentations

Ask for files **organised by category and named clearly**, with high-resolution and web versions.

---

## Usage rights

Confirm that the company can use the photos:
- in print and digital annual reports
- on the website and social media
- in investor and press materials
- for a set period, or without a time limit

Agree this in the quotation, especially if photos may appear in advertising.

## FAQ

### When should we schedule annual report photography?
Work back from the design deadline: brief 10 to 12 weeks before, shoot 6 to 8 weeks before, and deliver selected and retouched photos to the designer 4 to 6 weeks before. Book board and management portraits first.

### How do we photograph the board of directors efficiently?
Set up a portrait station next to the boardroom on a board meeting day, so directors can step out between agenda items. Plan 10 to 15 minutes per person and use one consistent style for all directors and management.

### Can we use stock photos in an annual report?
It's better not to. Real photos of your people, operations and initiatives are more credible to investors and stakeholders, especially in sustainability sections.

### What else can annual report photos be used for?
Website pages, press kits, recruitment, proposals, tenders, investor presentations and the sustainability report. Ask for files organised by category with high-resolution and web versions.

### How many shoot days does an annual report need?
Usually one to two days: one for board and management portraits, often on a board meeting day, and one for operations and people, depending on the number of locations.
