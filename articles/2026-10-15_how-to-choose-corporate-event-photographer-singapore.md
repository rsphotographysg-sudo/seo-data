# How to Choose a Corporate Event Photographer in Singapore

**Short answer:** The best corporate event photographer for you is the one who can prove six things:

- **full galleries** from events like yours, not just a highlight reel
- **backup** for people and equipment
- a **delivery time** that fits your plans
- experience with your **type of event and venue**
- the **compliance** your organisation needs: safety certification, PDPA handling and, where required, insurance
- a clear **nett quote** that states usage rights

Ask the 10 questions below before you book. The answers will separate a reliable vendor from a risky one faster than comparing prices.

*Written by Ricky Ee, Director of RS Photography. We have photographed corporate, MICE and government events in Singapore since 2013. This guide is meant to help you choose well, whoever you book.*

## Start with what the photos are for

Before you compare photographers, write down where the photos will be used. It decides what kind of photographer you need.

- **Social media during the event**: you need same-day delivery and an on-site editor.
- **Press release**: you need fast, well-captioned photos of the guest of honour and key speakers.
- **Annual report or brochure**: you need high-resolution, carefully retouched images and clear usage rights.
- **Sponsor reports**: you need booth and logo shots matched to each sponsor's contract.
- **Internal records**: you need complete coverage, and speed matters less.

A photographer who is excellent at weddings or portraits may not be the right fit for a 600-delegate conference with three parallel tracks. Match the vendor to the job.

## Freelancer, agency or marketplace?

| | Freelance photographer | Event photography company | Booking marketplace |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Typical pricing** | Hourly, often lower | Per half day or full day | Fixed packages per hour |
| **Backup if someone falls sick** | Usually depends on their network | A replacement from the same team | Depends on the platform |
| **Multiple photographers** | Must coordinate separate people | One team, one brief | Book each separately |
| **Same-day delivery** | Varies | Usually offered, with an on-site editor | Rarely |
| **Government paperwork** (GeBIZ, bizSAFE) | Varies | Often in place | Rarely |
| **Best for** | Small, simple events | Conferences, MICE, government and multi-day events | Short, straightforward shoots |

None of these is always the right answer. A freelancer can be a great choice for a two-hour networking session. For a multi-track conference or a government event, a team with backup and paperwork in place usually carries less risk.

## 10 questions to ask before you book

### 1. "Can I see a full gallery from an event like mine?"
Highlight reels show the 20 best photos from a hundred events. A **full gallery** from one comparable event shows what you will actually receive: consistent exposure, every speaker covered, no gaps during the programme.

### 2. "Have you worked at this venue before?"
Hotel ballrooms, convention centres and outdoor venues each have their own challenges: dark rooms, bright LED walls, mixed lighting, loading bay rules. Experience at your venue is a bonus, not a requirement, but ask how they would handle its lighting.

### 3. "What happens if your photographer falls sick on the day?"
This is the most important question. The answer should be a named replacement process, not "it has never happened".

### 4. "What backup equipment do you bring?"
Look for spare camera bodies, dual memory card slots and spare batteries. Cards are copied to a backup drive during the event.

### 5. "How many photographers do I need, and why?"
A good vendor will look at your programme and tell you where photographers are needed, not just sell you the biggest package. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/conference-photography-checklist "our conference photography checklist"].

### 6. "When will we receive the photos?"
Get it in writing: when the preview set arrives, when the full gallery arrives, and whether same-day selects are available. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/same-day-event-photo-delivery-singapore "how same-day delivery works"].

### 7. "What exactly is included in the price, and is GST added?"
Check that the quote lists hours, number of photographers, editing, delivery and travel. Ask whether GST is included or added on top. For typical budgets, see [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/corporate-event-photography-cost-singapore "corporate event photography costs in Singapore"].

### 8. "What rights do we get to use the photos?"
Marketing, annual reports, advertising and sponsor use should all be covered. If your contract requires full ownership, as many government contracts do, confirm this before booking.

### 9. "How do you handle guests who don't want to be photographed?"
The vendor should already know about the PDPA, opt-out markers and secure delivery. See our [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/pdpa-event-photography-consent-guide "PDPA event photography consent guide"].

### 10. "What certifications and insurance do you have?"
Some venues and organisations require public liability insurance or a safety certification such as bizSAFE. Government agencies may also need GeBIZ registration. Ask early, so you don't discover a gap a week before the event. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/government-event-photography-singapore "government event photography requirements"].

## Red flags to watch for

- **Only highlight photos, no full galleries.**
- **No plan for illness or equipment failure.**
- **A vague quote**, for example "event coverage – S$800" with no hours, editing or delivery time.
- **No written agreement** on delivery dates or usage rights.
- **Pressure to book immediately** without answering your questions.
- **Reviews that only mention weddings or portraits** when you need conference coverage.

---

## How to check reviews properly

- **Read the most recent reviews first.** Teams change over the years.
- **Look for reviews from events like yours**, such as conferences, galas or government functions.
- **Check reviews in more than one place**: Google, LinkedIn recommendations and any directory listings. The information should be consistent across them.
- **Ask for a reference** from a similar organisation if the event is high-stakes.

## A simple scoring sheet

Score each shortlisted vendor from 1 to 5 on:

| Criterion | What a 5 looks like |
|---|---|
| Relevant full galleries | Several full galleries from events like yours |
| Reliability | Named backup photographer and backup equipment |
| Delivery | Written timings that meet your needs |
| Compliance | Certifications, PDPA process and insurance your organisation needs |
| Clarity of quote | Every item listed, nett price, usage rights stated |
| Communication | Fast, clear answers to all 10 questions |

Choose on the total, not the price alone. The cheapest quote is often the most expensive if the guest of honour's arrival is missed.

## FAQ

### How do I choose the best corporate event photographer in Singapore?
Ask for full galleries from events like yours, check that the vendor has backup photographers and equipment, get delivery times and usage rights in writing, confirm the certifications your organisation needs, and compare itemised nett quotes rather than headline prices.

### Should I hire a freelancer or an event photography company?
A freelancer can suit small, simple events. For conferences, MICE events, government functions or anything with parallel sessions, a company with a team, backup photographers and same-day delivery usually carries less risk.

### What questions should I ask an event photographer before booking?
Ask to see a full gallery from a similar event, what happens if the photographer falls sick, what backup equipment they bring, how many photographers you need, when photos will be delivered, what the price includes, what usage rights you get, how they handle PDPA opt-outs, and what certifications and insurance they hold.

### How far in advance should we book an event photographer in Singapore?
Book four to eight weeks ahead for most corporate events, and earlier for September to December, when conferences, annual dinners and year-end events fill the calendar.

### What are the red flags when hiring an event photographer?
Only highlight photos and no full galleries, no backup plan for illness or equipment failure, vague quotes without hours or delivery times, no written agreement on usage rights, and reviews that don't include events like yours.
