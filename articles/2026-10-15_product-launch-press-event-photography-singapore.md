# Product Launch and Press Event Photography in Singapore

**Short answer:** At a product launch or press conference, the photos are part of the news. Plan for:

- **The unveil moment.** Shoot it from a clear, central position, with the product and the speaker in frame.
- **Product photos** taken before guests arrive, while the display is clean and well lit.
- **Press-ready delivery.** Selected photos, edited and **captioned with names and titles**, sent to your comms team **within hours**, so they can go out with the press release.

Agree the embargo time, the approval process and who sends photos to media before the day.

*Written by Ricky Ee, Director of RS Photography. We photograph launches, press conferences, signing ceremonies and media events in Singapore.*

## What makes a launch different

At most corporate events, photos are used afterwards. At a launch, they are used **the same day**, by media and on your own channels, often within a few hours. That changes the plan:

- **Timing matters more than volume.** Twenty strong, captioned photos within two hours beat 500 photos next week.
- **Every key frame has to work.** The unveil, the CEO with the product and the signing happen once and can't be repeated for the camera.
- **Media are in the room too.** Press photographers and video crews will compete for the same spots. Your photographer needs a reserved position.

## Launch and press event shot list

### Before guests arrive
- [ ] **Product hero shots** on the display: clean, well lit, from several angles
- [ ] The stage, backdrop and media wall with branding readable
- [ ] Venue and setup details: signage, demo stations, welcome area

### Arrival
- [ ] VIP and guest of honour arrival
- [ ] Media registration and guests at the media wall

### Programme
- [ ] Opening and speeches, with the backdrop and logo in frame
- [ ] **The unveil**: before, during and immediately after (curtain drop, button press or reveal)
- [ ] **The CEO or guest of honour with the product**, posed, facing the camera
- [ ] Demonstrations, with guests' reactions
- [ ] **Signing ceremonies**: the signing, the exchange of documents and the handshake facing the camera
- [ ] Media Q&A and interviews

### After the programme
- [ ] Guests trying the product
- [ ] Group photos: management, partners and guest of honour
- [ ] Influencer and media content moments, if planned

## Getting the unveil right

- **Rehearse the reveal** with the photographer present. Know exactly when the curtain drops or the screen changes.
- **Reserve a central position** in front of the stage for your photographer, separate from the media pen if possible.
- **Hold the moment.** Ask the speakers to pause with the product for a few seconds after the reveal, facing the cameras, before moving on.
- **Light the product.** Stage lighting is often set for speakers. Make sure the product is lit too, especially dark or reflective products.
- **Plan a second photographer** for reactions in the audience, which are often the most shareable shots.

## Press-ready photos: what media need

### Selection and editing
Send a **small, strong set**, usually 10–20 photos: the unveil, the CEO with the product, the signing, the guest of honour and a wide shot of the event.

### Captions
Media won't use photos they can't caption. For each photo, provide:
- who is in it, left to right, with **full names and titles**
- what is happening
- the event name, date and location

Prepare names and titles in advance so captions can be written on site.

### File formats
- **High-resolution** files for print and online news
- **Web-sized** files for social media and quick sharing
- Clear file names, for example `Launch-2026_CEO-unveil_01.jpg`

### Embargo and approval
- **Agree the embargo time** with your comms team. Photos should not be shared before the press release goes out.
- **Name one approver.** One person checks the selects before they go to media.
- **Decide who sends them**: your comms team or PR agency, not the photographer.

## Product photography at a launch

Launch-day product shots are useful, but they are not a replacement for **studio product photography** if you need catalogue or e-commerce images. At a launch:

- shoot products on the display **before doors open**, when they are clean and nobody is in the way
- shoot **in use**: hands on the product, demos and people interacting with it
- shoot **the product with people**: the CEO, guests and partners

For catalogue images with a plain background and exact colour, plan a separate studio session.

## FAQ

### How fast can we get photos for a press release after a launch?
With an on-site editor, a captioned set of 10 to 20 selected photos can be ready within about one to two hours of the key moment, in time to go out with a same-day press release.

### What photos do media want from a product launch?
The unveil moment, the CEO or guest of honour with the product, any signing ceremony, and a wide shot of the event. Each photo needs a caption with full names and titles, left to right.

### How do we make sure the unveil photo works?
Rehearse the reveal with the photographer, reserve a central position in front of the stage, light the product as well as the speakers, and ask speakers to pause facing the cameras for a few seconds after the reveal.

### Can launch photos replace studio product photography?
Not usually. Launch photos show the product in use and with people. Catalogue or e-commerce images with plain backgrounds and exact colour need a separate studio session.

### Who should send the photos to media?
Your communications team or PR agency, after one approver has checked the selection and the embargo time has passed. The photographer delivers the photos to your team, not directly to media.
