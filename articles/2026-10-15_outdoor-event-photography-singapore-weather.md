# Outdoor Event Photography in Singapore: Heat, Rain and Light

**Short answer:** Outdoor events in Singapore face three challenges: **sudden rain**, **harsh midday sun** and **heat**. Plan for them before the day:

- **Schedule key moments** for the morning or late afternoon, when the light is softer and it is cooler.
- **Prepare a wet-weather plan**: covered areas, an indoor backup and a clear decision time.
- **Watch for lightning**: outdoor activities should pause when there is lightning nearby.
- **Let cameras adjust** when moving from air-conditioned rooms to the outdoors, or lenses will fog.

*Written by Ricky Ee, Director of RS Photography. We photograph outdoor ceremonies, family days, runs, groundbreakings and community events across Singapore.*

## Singapore weather through the year

Singapore is hot and humid all year, but rain patterns change by season:

| Period | Typical weather | What it means for outdoor events |
|---|---|---|
| **December to early March** (Northeast Monsoon) | December and January are often the wettest months, with long periods of rain; February is usually drier and windier | Have a full indoor backup for December and January events |
| **March to May** (inter-monsoon) | Hot, with afternoon and early evening thunderstorms | Hold key moments in the morning |
| **June to September** (Southwest Monsoon) | Generally drier, with squalls that often arrive before dawn or in the morning | Mornings are usually fine but check early forecasts |
| **October to November** (inter-monsoon) | Afternoon and evening thunderstorms are common | Hold key moments in the morning; plan a rain backup |

Weather can change quickly at any time of year. Check the forecast from the **Meteorological Service Singapore** in the days before and on the morning of the event. [EXTERNAL LINK: Meteorological Service Singapore forecast page]

## Light: avoid the midday sun

Between about **11 am and 3 pm**, the sun is high and harsh. Faces get dark eye shadows and people squint.

- **Best light**: the first two hours after sunrise and the last two hours before sunset (around 7 pm in Singapore).
- **Use shade**: trees, building overhangs and tents give softer, even light.
- **Turn people away from the sun** for group photos, and use flash to fill in shadows.
- **Overcast days are good**: cloud gives soft, flattering light.

If the key moment must happen at midday, such as a groundbreaking with a VIP, ask for a **tent or canopy** over the stage area.

## Rain: the wet-weather plan

Agree this with the organiser before the day:

- **Covered areas**: where the programme moves if it rains (tents, covered walkways, a lobby)
- **An indoor backup**: for ceremonies and group photos
- **A decision time**: for example, decide by 2 hours before the start whether to move indoors
- **Who decides**: one person on the organiser's side
- **How guests are told**: MC, WhatsApp group or signage

### For the photographers
- **Rain covers** for cameras and lenses
- **Spare cloths** to dry equipment
- **A dry place** for spare equipment and laptops
- **Photos in the rain** can be great, with umbrellas and reactions, if the programme continues

---

## Lightning

Singapore has one of the highest rates of lightning in the world. During thunderstorms:

- **Pause outdoor activities**, especially in open fields, on rooftops and near tall structures.
- **Don't shelter under isolated trees.**
- **Stop drone flights.**
- **Follow the venue's lightning procedures**: many schools, sports facilities and parks have them.

Lightning alerts are available from the Meteorological Service Singapore and the myENV app. Assign one person on the organiser's team to watch them on the day.

---

## Heat and humidity

Heat affects guests, VIPs and the crew:

- **Shade and water** for guests, especially elderly guests and children
- **Short outdoor segments**: keep speeches and group photos brief
- **Tents for VIPs** during ceremonies
- **Crew breaks and water**: photographers carrying equipment in the sun need them too

### Camera fogging
Moving a cold camera from an air-conditioned room or car into the humid outdoors makes lenses **fog up**, sometimes for several minutes. Photographers should take equipment outside **15 to 30 minutes early**, or keep it in a bag to warm up gradually, so it is ready for the key moment.

## Planning outdoor key moments

| Moment | Tips |
|---|---|
| **Groundbreaking or ribbon-cutting** | Morning, with a canopy over the VIP area; site safety rules apply |
| **Large group photo** | Morning or late afternoon, in shade, people turned away from the sun |
| **Family day or carnival** | Roaming photographers, a shaded photo spot, a rain backup |
| **Run or race** | Early morning starts; lit areas for night races |
| **Outdoor dinner or reception** | Sunset for atmosphere, then flash and lighting after dark |

## Drones outdoors

A drone gives a striking aerial view of outdoor events. It needs a **licensed drone operator** and the right **permits**, and some areas in Singapore don't allow drones at all. Rain, strong wind and lightning stop drone flights, so always have a backup view from a high floor or rooftop.

## FAQ

### What is the best time of day for outdoor event photos in Singapore?
The first two hours after sunrise and the last two hours before sunset, around 7 pm. Avoid about 11 am to 3 pm, when the sun is harsh. Overcast days and shade also give soft, flattering light.

### When does it rain most in Singapore?
December and January are often the wettest months. In the inter-monsoon periods, March to May and October to November, afternoon and evening thunderstorms are common. Weather can change quickly at any time of year.

### What should a wet-weather plan for an outdoor event include?
Covered areas, an indoor backup for ceremonies and group photos, a decision time, one person who decides, and a way to tell guests. Photographers should bring rain covers and keep spare equipment dry.

### What should we do if there is lightning during an outdoor event?
Pause outdoor activities, especially in open fields, on rooftops and near tall structures, don't shelter under isolated trees, stop drone flights and follow the venue's lightning procedures. Assign someone to watch lightning alerts.

### Why do camera lenses fog up at outdoor events in Singapore?
Cold equipment from air-conditioned rooms fogs up in humid outdoor air. Photographers should take equipment outside 15 to 30 minutes early, or let it warm up gradually in a bag.
