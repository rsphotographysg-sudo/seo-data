# Who Owns Event Photos in Singapore? Copyright and Usage Rights

**Short answer:** Under Singapore's **Copyright Act 2021**, the **photographer is, by default, the copyright owner** of commissioned photos, unless the contract says otherwise. This applies to commissions under contracts made on or after **21 November 2021**. Before that, the client who commissioned the photos was usually the default owner.

In practice, most corporate clients don't need to own the copyright. They need **clear usage rights**: where, how and for how long they may use the photos. **Agree these in writing in the quotation**, including use by sponsors and partners, paid advertising, and the photographer's own portfolio.

*Written by Ricky Ee, Director of RS Photography. This article is general information, not legal advice. For a specific contract, check the Act or ask a lawyer.*

---

## What changed in 2021

| | Contracts before 21 November 2021 | Contracts on or after 21 November 2021 |
|---|---|---|
| **Default owner of commissioned photos** | Usually the client who commissioned and paid for them | The photographer (the creator) |
| **Can the contract change this?** | Yes | Yes |

The default only matters when the **contract says nothing** about ownership. If your quotation or contract states who owns the copyright, or what you may use the photos for, that agreement applies.

**Employees are different.** Photos taken by your own staff as part of their job generally belong to the employer.

## Ownership or a licence: what do you need?

| Option | What it means | When it makes sense |
|---|---|---|
| **Usage licence** | The photographer keeps the copyright; you may use the photos as agreed | Most corporate events: websites, social media, press, reports and marketing |
| **Exclusive licence** | As above, but the photographer may not license the photos to anyone else | Confidential events, product launches before release, or sensitive clients |
| **Copyright assignment** | The copyright is transferred to you in writing | When your procurement rules or the project require it; often priced higher |

For most organisations, a **broad, permanent business-use licence** gives everything they need, without paying for an assignment.

## The usage rights to agree

Before the event, agree in the quotation:

- **Channels**: website, social media, newsletters, internal comms, press, print, annual reports
- **Paid advertising**: online ads, billboards and printed ads are often treated separately
- **Duration**: permanent, or for a fixed period
- **Territory**: Singapore only, or worldwide (online use is worldwide in practice)
- **Third parties**: whether sponsors, partners, speakers, venues and media may use the photos, and on what terms
- **Editing**: whether you may crop, retouch or add text and logos
- **Credit**: whether a photo credit is required, and how it should appear

---

## Sponsors, speakers and partners

This is where most misunderstandings happen. If you plan to send photos to **sponsors, speakers, venues or partners**, check that your licence allows it:

- **Sponsors** often want to use photos of their booth and logo in their own marketing.
- **Speakers** post their photos on LinkedIn and on their own websites.
- **Venues and hotels** may want photos of your event for their marketing.
- **Media** may publish photos you send with a press release.

Agree in the quotation that you may **share photos with these parties for their own non-commercial or promotional use**, or ask them to contact the photographer directly. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/how-to-use-corporate-event-photos "how to use your corporate event photos"].

## The photographer's portfolio

Photographers usually want to show some photos in their portfolio, website and social media. If your event is **confidential**, involves **VIPs or sensitive guests**, or shows **unreleased products**, say so in advance and agree that the photos will **not be used publicly** by the photographer, or only after a date you approve.

---

## Copyright is not consent

Owning or licensing the photos **does not mean you can use every person in them for any purpose**. The people in the photos still have rights:

- The **PDPA** applies to photos of identifiable people. Tell guests about photography and offer an opt-out.
- **Paid advertising** using an identifiable guest needs extra care, and usually their consent.
- **Children** should only appear publicly with parents' agreement.

## Government and procurement contracts

Government agencies and large organisations often have **standard terms on intellectual property** in their quotation or tender documents. Some require **assignment of copyright** to the agency, or a broad licence for any government use. If you are buying for an agency, state the IP terms you need in the quotation documents, so vendors can price them correctly. ## FAQ

### Who owns the copyright in event photos in Singapore?
Under the Copyright Act 2021, the photographer is the default owner of commissioned photos under contracts made on or after 21 November 2021, unless the contract says otherwise. The client and photographer can agree a different arrangement in writing.

### Do we need to own the copyright in our event photos?
Usually not. Most organisations only need a clear usage licence that covers their website, social media, press, reports and marketing, permanently. Copyright assignment is mainly needed when procurement rules or a project require it.

### Can we give our event photos to sponsors and speakers?
Only if your licence allows it. Agree in the quotation that you may share photos with sponsors, speakers, partners and media for their own promotional use, or ask them to contact the photographer.

### Can the photographer use our event photos in their portfolio?
Often yes, unless agreed otherwise. If the event is confidential, involves sensitive guests or shows unreleased products, agree in advance that the photographer will not use the photos publicly, or only after a date you approve.

### If we have the usage rights, can we use any guest's photo in an advert?
Not automatically. Usage rights cover the photos, but the people in them still have rights under the PDPA. Using an identifiable guest in paid advertising needs extra care, and usually their consent.

## Summary

For corporate event photos in Singapore:

- since the **Copyright Act 2021**, the photographer is the **default copyright owner** unless the contract says otherwise
- most clients need **clear usage rights**, not ownership
- agree **channels, paid ads, duration, third parties and portfolio use** in writing
- remember that **copyright is not consent**: the PDPA still applies
- check **IP terms** in government and procurement contracts
