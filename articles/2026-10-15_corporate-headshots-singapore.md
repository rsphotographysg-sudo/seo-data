# Corporate Headshots in Singapore: A Guide to On-Site Team Photos

**Short answer:** For most companies, the easiest way to get consistent corporate headshots is an **on-site headshot session**. The photographer sets up a small studio in a meeting room, and staff come in **5-minute slots**, so a photographer can do about **10–12 people an hour**.

Agree on these before the shoot:

- **background**
- **lighting**
- **crop**
- **retouching level**

Then record the setup, so **new joiners photographed months later match** the rest of the team.

## What kind of photos do you need?

| Type | What it is | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Headshot** | Head and shoulders, plain or branded background | LinkedIn, website team page, email signatures, ID passes |
| **Executive portrait** | More time per person, several looks, posed with care | Annual reports, press kits, speaker profiles, board pages |
| **Environmental portrait** | Person in their workplace: office, lab, factory floor | About pages, recruitment, articles and interviews |
| **Team photo** | Department or whole company together | Website, recruitment, internal comms |

Many companies combine these in one day: headshots for everyone, plus executive portraits for the leadership team.

---

## On-site or in a studio?

**On-site (in your office)** works best for most companies:

- Staff leave their desk for 5–10 minutes, not half a day.
- Large teams can be done in one or two days.
- Office backgrounds can be used for environmental portraits.

**A studio** suits small groups and executives:

- Full control of lighting and backgrounds.
- More privacy and time for senior leaders.
- No meeting room needed.

For on-site sessions you need a room of at least about **4 m × 4 m**, ideally with blinds to control daylight, and a power point.

---

## How to plan the day

### 1. Count people and set slots
Plan **5 minutes per person** for headshots, and **15–30 minutes** for executive portraits. That gives about **10–12 headshots per hour**, or roughly **60–70 people in a full day** with breaks.

### 2. Use a booking sheet
Share a sign-up sheet with 5-minute slots. Keep a few spare slots each hour for latecomers and for people who want a retake.

### 3. Send a short brief to staff
Tell staff the time, place, what to wear and that they can check their photo before leaving. People relax more when they know what to expect.

### 4. Agree on the look in advance
Approve one sample photo before the day:
- background colour
- lighting style (bright and even, or more contrast)
- crop (head and shoulders, or half body)

### 5. Name files clearly
Ask for photos named by person, for example `Tan-Mei-Ling_Finance.jpg`, so HR and IT can update profiles quickly.

## Keep the look consistent across the company

The biggest problem with company headshots is **inconsistency**: people photographed at different times by different photographers, with different backgrounds and lighting. On a website team page, it looks untidy.

To avoid it:

- **Pick one background**: a plain grey, white or brand colour, or one office location.
- **Use the same lighting setup and lens** for everyone.
- **Use the same crop and eye-line height**, so heads line up on a team page.
- **Record the setup**: lighting positions, camera settings, background and crop. Use it for every future session.

### Plan for new joiners

New staff join throughout the year. The cheapest way to keep them consistent is a **regular "new joiner" session**, for example once a quarter, using the recorded setup. One person at a time is expensive. Six people every three months is not.

## What should staff wear?

Send this to staff a few days before:

- **Solid colours** work best. Avoid busy patterns and fine stripes, which can look distorted on camera.
- **Wear what you wear for important meetings.** Suit and tie are not required unless your company style calls for it.
- **Avoid the same colour as the background**, for example a grey top on a grey background.
- **Bring a jacket** if you might want a more formal version.
- **Check glasses**: clean lenses and, if possible, no tint.
- **Keep it simple**: minimal jewellery, nothing with large logos.

For uniformed staff or customer-facing roles, follow the company's uniform guidelines.

## Retouching: how much is right?

Agree on the level in advance, so every photo matches:

- **Basic** (standard): colour, exposure, crop, removing stray hairs and temporary blemishes.
- **Enhanced**: softer skin and more cleanup, for executive portraits and print.
- **What not to do**: change face shape, body shape or features. Headshots should look like the person who turns up to the meeting.

### What about AI headshots?

AI-generated headshots are cheap and fast, but for companies they have drawbacks:

- results can look slightly unlike the real person
- consistency across a team is hard to control
- some clients and partners see them as less trustworthy

For a team page, a professional photo session usually gives a more consistent and credible result.

## Usage rights and privacy

- **Usage.** Confirm the company can use the photos on the website, LinkedIn, proposals, press materials and ID passes.
- **Staff consent.** Staff photos are personal data. Tell staff how the photos will be used, and remove photos of people who leave the company from the website. See our [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/pdpa-event-photography-consent-guide "PDPA guide for photography"].
- **Storage.** Keep the original files in a shared company folder, not on one person's laptop.

## FAQ

### How many headshots can be taken in an hour?
About 10 to 12 people an hour, using 5-minute slots. A full day on site, with breaks, typically covers 60 to 70 people. Executive portraits take longer, usually 15 to 30 minutes each.

### Is it better to do corporate headshots on-site or in a studio?
On-site is usually better for teams, because staff only leave their desk for a few minutes and large groups can be done in a day. A studio suits small groups and executive portraits that need more time and privacy.

### How do we keep headshots consistent when new staff join?
Use the same background, lighting, lens and crop for everyone, record the setup, and hold regular new-joiner sessions, for example every quarter, using that same setup.

### What should staff wear for corporate headshots?
Solid colours, clothing they would wear to an important meeting, and a colour that contrasts with the background. Avoid busy patterns, large logos and tinted glasses.

### Are AI headshots a good alternative?
They are cheaper and faster, but they can look unlike the real person, are hard to keep consistent across a team, and may seem less trustworthy to clients. A professional session gives a more consistent and credible team page.
