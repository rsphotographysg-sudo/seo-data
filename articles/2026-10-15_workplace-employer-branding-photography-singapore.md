# Workplace and Employer Branding Photography in Singapore

**Short answer:** Candidates and clients want to see **real people at real work**, not stock photos. A workplace shoot gives you photos for your website, careers page, LinkedIn and recruitment ads. Plan it in one or two days and cover four things:

- **people at work**: meetings, collaboration, focused work, customer moments
- **the workplace**: office, studio, lab, warehouse or shop floor
- **team photos**: departments and the whole company
- **culture moments**: lunches, celebrations, training and volunteering

Use **your own staff, with their consent**, plan a **shot list by department**, and brief everyone a week before so the office and people are ready.

## Why not use stock photos?

- **Candidates notice.** Stock photos look generic, and the same images appear on other companies' sites.
- **Trust.** Clients want to see the people they will work with.
- **Consistency.** One shoot gives a matching set of photos across your website, LinkedIn and recruitment materials.
- **Recognition.** Staff like seeing themselves and their teams on the company's channels, and often share the posts.

## Planning the shoot

### A shot list by department
Ask each department head for two or three activities that show what the team actually does. Group them into a timed schedule, so each team is photographed for 20–30 minutes and then goes back to work.

### Choose a normal working day
A busy, real day looks better than an empty office. Avoid public holidays, month-end closing and days when half the team works from home.

### Brief staff a week before
- what the photos are for and where they will appear
- the schedule for their team
- what to wear: normal work clothes, avoiding large logos and busy patterns
- how to opt out

### Prepare the space
- clear desks of clutter and confidential papers
- tidy cables, whiteboards and the pantry
- check screens show nothing confidential
- book the meeting rooms you want to use

## Making it look natural

- **Real tasks.** Ask people to do what they normally do, rather than pose with fake smiles.
- **Small groups.** Two to four people talking over a laptop or whiteboard looks natural and is easy to arrange.
- **Diversity.** Show the real mix of your team: ages, roles, backgrounds and seniority, not only the youngest staff.
- **Varied angles.** Wide shots of the space, medium shots of groups, close-ups of hands and details.
- **Natural light.** Use window light where possible, with discreet lighting added for darker rooms.

---

## Consent and privacy

Workplace photos show identifiable staff, so plan consent properly:

- **Ask for written consent** for photos used on the website, recruitment ads and social media.
- **Offer an opt-out**, and photograph those staff out of frame or not at all.
- **Agree what happens when people leave.** Decide in advance whether photos of former staff are removed, and when.
- **Check screens and documents** for client names and confidential data.
- **Ask clients and visitors** before including them in any photo.

## Building a photo library

One shoot should give you photos for the whole year:

- **Wide and vertical crops** for website banners, LinkedIn and Instagram stories
- **Space for text** in some photos, for ads and careers page headers
- **Folders by use**: careers, about page, departments, culture
- **A refresh plan**: update every one to two years, or after an office move or rebrand

## FAQ

### Why use real workplace photos instead of stock photos?
Real photos show candidates and clients the actual people and workplace, build trust, and give a consistent set of images across the website, LinkedIn and recruitment materials. Stock photos look generic and often appear on other companies' sites.

### What should a workplace photo shoot include?
People at work, the workplace itself, department and company team photos, culture moments such as celebrations and training, and environmental portraits of individual staff in their roles.

### How do we make workplace photos look natural?
Photograph real tasks in small groups of two to four people, choose a normal busy working day, use window light where possible, show the real mix of the team, and vary wide, medium and close-up shots.

### Do we need staff consent for workplace photos?
Yes. Get written consent for photos used on the website, in recruitment ads and on social media, offer an opt-out, and agree in advance what happens to photos of staff who leave.

### How long does a workplace photo shoot take?
Usually a half day for a small office and a full day for several departments or sites, with 20 to 30 minutes per team. Headshots or a short video can be added on the same day.
