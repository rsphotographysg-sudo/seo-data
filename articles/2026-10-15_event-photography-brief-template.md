# Event Photography Brief Template: What to Send Your Photographer

**Short answer:** A good photography brief fits on **one or two pages** and answers ten questions:

1. **What** is the event, and **when and where** is it?
2. **Who** is the contact on the day?
3. **Why** do you need photos, and where will they be used?
4. **What happens when**: the run-of-show
5. **Which moments** must not be missed?
6. **Who** are the VIPs, speakers and guests of honour?
7. **What branding** and sponsors must appear?
8. **Who must not** be photographed?
9. **What logistics** apply: arrival, parking, dress code, meals?
10. **When and how** should the photos be delivered, and with what usage rights?

Send it **at least a week before** the event. Copy the template below.

## Tips for each section

### Event and contacts
Give **one decision-maker** on the day. When a VIP arrives early or the programme changes, the photographer needs someone who can answer quickly.

### Purpose and use
This is the most important section. The same event is photographed differently for **press** (a few clean, captioned shots), **social media** (vertical crops, reactions, speed) or an **annual report** (high resolution, leadership on stage). See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/how-to-use-corporate-event-photos "how to use your corporate event photos"].

### Run-of-show and must-have moments
Mark the moments that **only happen once**: the guest of honour's arrival, a ribbon-cutting, an award, a lo hei toss. Give times, and tell the photographer if they change.

### VIPs and speakers
**Photos of VIPs and speakers** help the photographer recognise them in a crowd. For awards, a running order of recipients lets the photographer name photos per person. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/award-ceremony-photography-singapore "award ceremony photography"].

### Branding and sponsors
List what each sponsor needs, so their photos can be delivered in a separate folder.

### Privacy
Tell the photographer how opt-outs are marked, and list anything confidential. See our [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/pdpa-event-photography-consent-guide "PDPA event photography consent guide"].

### Delivery and usage
Agree delivery times and **usage rights** in writing before the event. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/same-day-event-photo-delivery-singapore "same-day event photo delivery"] and [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/event-photo-copyright-usage-rights-singapore "event photo copyright and usage rights"].

## Common brief mistakes

- **No purpose**: "take nice photos" doesn't tell the photographer what matters.
- **An outdated programme**: send the final version, and flag late changes.
- **No VIP photos**: the photographer can't recognise people from names alone.
- **No group photo plan**: who, where and when. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/large-group-photo-corporate-event-singapore "large group photos"].
- **Forgetting sponsors** until after the event.
- **Delivery and usage rights not agreed** in advance.

## FAQ

### What should an event photography brief include?
Event details and contacts, the purpose and use of the photos, the run-of-show, must-have moments, VIPs and speakers with photos, branding and sponsors, privacy and opt-outs, logistics such as arrival and dress code, and delivery times and usage rights.

### When should we send the brief to the photographer?
At least a week before the event, with the final run-of-show. Flag any late changes to the programme or VIP list as soon as you know them.

### Why does the photographer need to know how the photos will be used?
Because the event is photographed differently for press, social media, annual reports or sponsors. Knowing the use decides the shots, crops, resolution and delivery timing.

### Should we send photos of VIPs to the photographer?
Yes. Photos or LinkedIn links help the photographer recognise VIPs and speakers in a crowd, and a running order lets them name photos per person.

### Is there a free event photography brief template?
Yes. The template on this page can be copied into an email or document. It covers event details, contacts, purpose, run-of-show, must-have moments, VIPs, branding, privacy, logistics, delivery and usage rights.
