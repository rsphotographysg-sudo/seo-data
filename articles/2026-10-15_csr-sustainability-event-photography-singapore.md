# CSR and Sustainability Event Photography in Singapore

**Short answer:** CSR and sustainability events, such as **tree planting, beach clean-ups, recycling drives, food packing and volunteering**, are photographed for two audiences: **staff and social media** today, and **sustainability (ESG) reports** later. Good photos:

- show **real activity and real impact**, not just posed group photos
- include **evidence**: before and after, quantities collected, trees planted, people reached
- come with **accurate captions**: date, place, partner and what was achieved
- respect **beneficiaries' consent** and dignity
- are taken **safely**, outdoors in Singapore's heat and rain

*Written by Ricky Ee, Director of RS Photography. We photograph corporate, community and outdoor events in Singapore.*

## Show real impact

Sustainability reports and readers increasingly look for **evidence**, not just smiles.

- **Before and after**: the beach or site before the clean-up and after.
- **Quantities**: bags collected, boxes packed, trees planted, with a sign or count visible.
- **Process**: sorting, weighing, planting and packing, showing what was done.
- **Partners**: the charity, community group or agency you worked with.
- **The team**: everyone who took part, at the end.

---

## Accurate captions for ESG reports

Photos used in sustainability reports need **accurate, specific information**:

- **Date and location**
- **Activity and partner organisation**
- **What was achieved**: quantities, numbers of volunteers, hours
- **Who is in the photo**, if named

**Photos should honestly represent what happened.** Avoid staging that suggests more impact than was achieved; misleading sustainability claims can damage trust.

## Beneficiaries and consent

When CSR activities involve people being helped:

- **Get consent** through the partner organisation before photographing beneficiaries.
- **Show dignity**: people as participants, not as recipients to be pitied.
- **Children and vulnerable people**: only with guardian consent, and often better not identifiable.
- **Focus on your team at work** when consent is not possible.

## Outdoors and safety

Many CSR events are outdoors, in parks, beaches and coastal areas:

- **Heat and rain**: plan shade, water and a wet-weather plan. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/outdoor-event-photography-singapore-weather "outdoor event photography in Singapore weather"].
- **Early starts**: morning light is softer and cooler.
- **Safety**: photographers follow the organiser's safety briefing, such as gloves, sun protection and tide times for beaches.
- **Protected areas**: some nature areas have rules on access and photography, and drones need permits.

## FAQ

### What should be photographed at a CSR event?
Real activity and real impact: the team at work, before and after the site, quantities collected or packed, partner organisations, and a team photo at the end, with consent for any beneficiaries.

### How should CSR photos be captioned for ESG reports?
With the date, location, activity, partner organisation and what was achieved, such as quantities, number of volunteers and hours, plus names where people are identified.

### Can we stage photos for a sustainability report?
Photos should honestly represent what happened. Avoid staging that suggests more impact than was achieved, because misleading sustainability claims can damage trust.

### Can beneficiaries be photographed at CSR events?
Only with consent arranged through the partner organisation, shown with dignity. For children and vulnerable people, guardian consent is needed and they are often better not identifiable.

### When are CSR events best photographed?
Usually in the morning, when light is softer and it is cooler, with shade, water, a wet-weather plan and the organiser's safety briefing followed.
