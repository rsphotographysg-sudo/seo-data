# Non-Profit and Charity Photography and Video in Singapore

**Short answer:** For non-profits, charities and social service agencies, photos and videos have to do three jobs at once:

- **show impact** to donors and funders
- **thank volunteers and partners**
- **respect the people you serve**

Plan around **beneficiary dignity and consent first**, then shoot so one session produces material for **several uses**: the annual report, donor updates, social media, grant reports and recruitment. That is how a limited budget goes furthest.

## Beneficiary dignity and consent

This matters more than anything else on a non-profit shoot.

- **Consent first, always.** Every identifiable beneficiary gives informed consent. For children, people with intellectual disabilities and some elderly beneficiaries, consent comes from a parent, guardian or caregiver, following your organisation's policy.
- **Explain the use.** Tell people clearly where their image will appear (annual report, social media, a fundraising video) and that they can say no or withdraw later.
- **Show strength, not pity.** Photograph people taking part, learning, working and connecting, not as helpless recipients. Avoid images that would embarrass someone if their family or employer saw them.
- **Protect identities when needed.** Shoot from behind, use hands and activities instead of faces, or keep people out of focus when consent can't be given or the topic is sensitive.
- **Watch the details.** Names on lanyards, home addresses, case files and medical information must not appear.
- **Have staff present.** A staff member who knows the beneficiaries should be at every programme shoot.

## Fundraising galas and charity events

Galas are planned like corporate dinners, with a few extra priorities:

- **Donors and sponsors**: photograph major donors and sponsors with the guest of honour and the organisation's leaders. Check that each sponsor's logo appears where it was promised.
- **The appeal moment**: the impact video screening, the pledge or the cheque presentation.
- **Beneficiary performances**: plan these carefully with consent and staff support. They are often the most powerful moments of the night.
- **Auctions and lucky draws**: winners with their items.

## Volunteer events

Volunteers are often the people who share photos most. Make it easy:

- **Photograph every volunteer group or team**, especially corporate volunteer teams. Their companies often want photos for their own sustainability or CSR reports.
- **Deliver quickly.** Volunteers post the day after, not three weeks later.
- **Show the work being done**: packing, teaching, cleaning, visiting. Not only posed group photos.

## One shoot, many uses

Non-profit budgets are tight, so plan each shoot to cover several needs:

| One programme shoot can supply | |
|---|---|
| Annual report photos | Programme and impact images |
| Donor updates and newsletters | Recent activity photos |
| Grant reports | Proof that the programme ran, with participants and activities |
| Social media | Short clips and candid photos |
| Volunteer recruitment | Volunteers in action |
| Website | Updated programme photos |

Tell the photographer about all these uses in the brief, so they can shoot horizontal and vertical frames, wide and close shots, and short video clips during the same session.

## Campaign and impact videos

A good non-profit video tells **one person's story** clearly, rather than listing every programme.

- **Choose the storyteller carefully**: a beneficiary, caregiver, volunteer or staff member who is comfortable on camera and has given full consent.
- **Keep it short**: 60–120 seconds for social media and gala screenings, with shorter cutdowns.
- **End with a clear ask**: donate, volunteer or share.
- **Add subtitles**, since most people watch with the sound off.

## Stretching the budget

- **Combine shoots.** Photograph a programme, a volunteer session and staff portraits on the same day.
- **Book photo and video together** with one crew. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/event-photography-and-videography-singapore "event photography and videography from one crew"].
- **Plan a year in advance.** A small set of shoot days across the year, agreed upfront, often costs less than booking each event separately.
- **Ask about funding.** Some grants for capability building or campaigns can cover photography and video. Check the conditions with your funder.

## FAQ

### How do non-profits get consent to photograph beneficiaries?
Every identifiable beneficiary gives informed consent, or a parent, guardian or caregiver gives it for children and people who can't consent themselves. Explain where the images will be used and that people can say no or withdraw later.

### How can we show impact without exposing vulnerable beneficiaries?
Photograph activities, hands and interactions instead of faces, shoot from behind or out of focus, and remove names, addresses and case details. Focus on people taking part, not on hardship.

### What should we photograph at a fundraising gala?
Major donors and sponsors with the guest of honour and leaders, sponsor logos where promised, the appeal moment or cheque presentation, beneficiary performances with consent, and auction or lucky draw winners.

### How can a non-profit get more value from a photography budget?
Plan each shoot for several uses (annual report, donor updates, grant reports, social media and recruitment), combine programme, volunteer and portrait shoots on the same day, and book photo and video together.

### How long should a fundraising video be?
Usually 60 to 120 seconds for social media and gala screenings, with shorter cutdowns. Tell one person's story clearly and end with a clear ask to donate, volunteer or share.
