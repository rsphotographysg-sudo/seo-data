# Embassy and Diplomatic Reception Photography in Singapore

**Short answer:** Embassy and high commission events, such as **national day receptions**, ministerial visits and cultural evenings, follow **protocol**. The photography has to follow it too:

- **Photograph every guest in the receiving line** with the ambassador, and keep a record of who is in each photo.
- **Follow the order of precedence** for group photos and seating.
- **Stay still during national anthems**, and keep flags correctly displayed and fully in frame.
- **Plan for security**: names, IDs and equipment lists sent in advance.
- **Deliver captioned photos the same night** for the embassy's social media and press release.

## The receiving line

At a national day reception, the ambassador and spouse often greet every guest at the entrance. These handshake photos are among the most requested.

- **Fixed position**: one photographer stays at the receiving line for its full duration, with consistent framing and lighting.
- **Every guest, every time**: guests expect their photo with the ambassador, and diplomats often share them.
- **Keep a record**: note the order of guests or photograph name cards, so photos can be matched to names later.
- **Keep the line moving**: one or two frames per guest, without slowing the greeting.
- **A second photographer** covers the room, arrivals of VIPs and the guest of honour.

---

## Protocol and precedence

- **Get the order of precedence** from the protocol officer: who stands where in group photos and who is photographed with whom.
- **Use correct titles in captions**: for example, "His Excellency" or "Her Excellency" for ambassadors, and the exact titles of ministers and officials.
- **Signing ceremonies**: photograph the signing, the exchange of documents and the handshake, without readable pages.
- **Bilateral meetings**: often only a short photo opportunity at the start. Be ready and leave when asked.

## Anthems and flags

- **Stand still during national anthems.** Photograph from a fixed position, with no movement or flash, or not at all if the organiser prefers.
- **Flags**: keep them clean, upright and fully visible. Avoid cropping a flag or showing it in an unflattering way.
- **Flag order**: follow the arrangement agreed by the embassy, especially when two countries' flags are shown together.

---

## Security and accreditation

Embassy events, ministerial visits and residences have security requirements:

- **Send names, ID details and the equipment list** in advance, as requested.
- **Arrive early** for security checks.
- **Stay in agreed areas**: some rooms and the residence's private areas are off-limits.
- **Discretion**: no personal social media posting, and no sharing of guest lists or photos outside the agreed channels.

---

## Captions and delivery

Diplomatic missions usually post on the same night or the next morning.

- **Same-night selects**: the ambassador's speech, the guest of honour, the toast and key guests, captioned with names and titles.
- **Receiving line photos**: delivered in a gallery or folders, matched to names where possible, so the embassy can send them to guests.
- **Full gallery**: within a few working days.

## Privacy and consent

- **Tell guests** about photography on the invitation or at the entrance.
- **Respect requests not to be photographed**, especially from guests in sensitive roles.
- **Agree how receiving line photos are shared** with guests.

## FAQ

### How do you photograph a receiving line at a national day reception?
With one photographer at a fixed position for the whole receiving line, consistent framing and lighting, one or two frames per guest, and a record of the order of guests so photos can be matched to names. A second photographer covers the room and the guest of honour.

### What protocol should event photographers follow at embassy events?
Follow the order of precedence from the protocol officer for group photos, use correct titles such as His or Her Excellency in captions, stay still during national anthems, keep flags fully and correctly displayed, and leave bilateral meetings when the photo opportunity ends.

### What security steps are needed to photograph an embassy event?
Send names, ID details and the equipment list in advance as requested, arrive early for security checks, stay in agreed areas, and keep guest lists and photos within the agreed channels.

### How quickly do embassies need event photos?
Usually the same night or the next morning, for social media and press releases. Same-night selects are captioned with names and titles, and the full gallery follows within a few working days.

### How many photographers does a national day reception need?
Usually two: one at the receiving line for its full duration, and one covering arrivals, the guest of honour, speeches, the toast and the room.
