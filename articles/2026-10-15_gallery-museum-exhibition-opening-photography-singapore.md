# Gallery and Museum Exhibition Opening Photography in Singapore

**Short answer:** An exhibition opening has two different photography jobs:

- **the event**: guests engaging with the works, the artist and curator, speeches, the guest of honour and the atmosphere
- **installation views**: clean photos of the exhibition itself, usually taken **before the opening** when the galleries are empty

Agree with the gallery or museum on **flash rules near artworks**, **which works may be photographed and published**, and how **artists' copyright** is respected in the photos.

*Written by Ricky Ee, Director of RS Photography. We photograph launches, venues and cultural events in Singapore.*

## Photographing the event

- **People with the works**: guests looking, discussing and photographing artworks. These show engagement better than either alone.
- **The artist and curator**: with guests, explaining works, and a portrait with a key piece.
- **Speeches and the opening moment**: the guest of honour, the director and the artist.
- **Atmosphere**: wide shots of a busy gallery, drinks and conversation.
- **Sponsors and partners** with the exhibition and its branding.

## Installation views

Installation views are the record of the exhibition, used by the gallery, artist and press for years.

- **Shoot before the opening**, when the galleries are empty and clean.
- **Use a tripod** and the gallery's own lighting, so colours and atmosphere are accurate.
- **Straight lines**: walls and plinths should look true.
- **Wide views of each room** plus **individual works** and details.
- **Check colour accuracy** against the works, especially for paintings and prints.

---

## Light and flash near artworks

- **Ask about flash**: many galleries and museums don't allow flash near certain works, especially light-sensitive ones.
- **Use the exhibition lighting**: it is designed for the works and creates the right mood.
- **Fast lenses and careful settings** handle dim galleries.
- **Avoid reflections** on glass and glossy surfaces by choosing angles carefully.

---

## Copyright in artworks

Artworks are usually protected by copyright, separately from the photographs:

- **Agree with the gallery or museum** which works may appear in event photos and installation views, and how they may be published.
- **Credit artists** as the gallery requests, with the artwork title and details.
- **Event photos** that show works in the background are usually fine for the gallery's own channels, but check before using them in advertising.
- **Installation views** used commercially may need the artist's permission.

## FAQ

### What should be photographed at an exhibition opening?
Guests engaging with the works, the artist and curator with guests, speeches and the opening moment, the atmosphere of a busy gallery, and sponsors with the exhibition. Installation views are usually photographed separately before the opening.

### When should installation views be photographed?
Before the opening, when the galleries are empty and clean, using a tripod and the gallery's lighting for accurate colour and straight lines.

### Can photographers use flash in galleries and museums?
Often not near certain works, especially light-sensitive ones. Ask the gallery or museum first, and use the exhibition lighting with fast lenses and careful settings.

### Do artworks in event photos raise copyright issues?
Artworks are usually protected by copyright. Agree with the gallery or museum which works may appear and how photos may be published, credit artists as requested, and check before using photos of works in advertising.

### How long does exhibition opening photography take?
Usually two to three hours for the opening, plus a separate session before the opening for installation views, priced by the number of rooms and works.
