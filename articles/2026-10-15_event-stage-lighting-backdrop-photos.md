# Event Stage Lighting and Backdrops That Photograph Well

**Short answer:** Most disappointing event photos come from the **stage setup**, not the photographer. Before the event, ask your AV team and designer for:

- **Front light on speakers' faces**, brighter than the screen or LED wall behind them
- **One colour of light** on stage (avoid mixing warm and cool), with coloured effects kept off faces
- **A lectern placed to the side**, not in front of the screen, at a height that doesn't hide short speakers
- **A matte backdrop** with the logo at **head height and above**, not at floor level
- **House lights raised** for awards, speeches and audience photos at dinners

These cost little to arrange in advance and are hard to fix on the day.

## Light the faces, not just the stage

The most common problem: **speakers look dark against a bright screen**.

- **Front light** (from the front, above the audience) should light the speaker's face evenly.
- **The face should be at least as bright as the screen** or LED wall behind. If the screen is very bright, ask the AV team to lower its brightness during speeches.
- **Avoid only top light**: it puts dark shadows in eye sockets.
- **Light the whole stage area** where people stand during panels, awards and handshakes, not only the lectern.

---

## LED walls and screens

- **Brightness**: LED walls are often set very bright. A slightly lower setting during speeches helps faces and photos.
- **Content during key moments**: show the event logo or a clean slide during awards and handshakes, not busy video.
- **Flicker and lines**: some LED walls show lines in photos at certain camera settings. Experienced photographers adjust; a quick test during rehearsal helps.

## Colour of light

- **One colour temperature** on faces: mixing warm (yellow) and cool (blue) lights makes skin tones look strange.
- **Coloured effects** (purple, red, blue washes) look good on the room but bad on faces. Keep them for walk-ins and performances, not speeches.
- **Ask for "white" stage light during speeches and awards**, and save the colour for the show moments.

---

## Lectern and stage layout

- **Put the lectern to the side** of the screen, angled towards the audience.
- **Check the lectern height**: tall lecterns hide shorter speakers. A step or a lower lectern helps.
- **Keep microphones and laptops low**, so they don't cover faces.
- **Mark positions** for awards and handshakes, facing the main camera position. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/award-ceremony-photography-singapore "award ceremony photography"].
- **Keep the stage clear** of cables, empty bottles and boxes.

---

## Backdrops and step-and-repeat walls

- **Matte material**: glossy backdrops reflect flash and stage lights as bright spots.
- **Logo height**: put the main logo at **head height and above**, so it appears behind people's heads in photos, not hidden by bodies.
- **Repeat the logo** across the width on step-and-repeat walls, so every crop includes it.
- **Simple design**: busy patterns and fine text don't read in photos.
- **Size**: wide enough for group photos, usually at least 3 m for a group of 8 to 10 people.
- **Light the backdrop evenly**, without hot spots.

## Room and house lights at dinners

Dinners are often dimmed for atmosphere, which makes photos of guests difficult.

- **Raise the house lights** a little during speeches, awards and table photos.
- **Light the tables**: candles alone are not enough for photos.
- **Agree the cues** with the AV team: lights up for awards, down for performances.

## What to ask your AV team

- [ ] Front light on speakers' faces, brighter than the screen
- [ ] White light on stage during speeches and awards
- [ ] LED wall brightness lowered during speeches
- [ ] Clean slides or logo during key moments
- [ ] Lectern to the side, at a suitable height
- [ ] House lights raised for awards and table photos
- [ ] A short lighting check with the photographer during rehearsal

## The photographer's part

A good event photographer adapts to difficult light, with fast lenses, flash where appropriate and careful settings. But **no camera can fix a face that is much darker than the screen behind it**, or a logo hidden behind people. A **ten-minute walkthrough** with the AV team before doors open solves most problems. ## FAQ

### Why do speakers look dark in event photos?
Because the screen or LED wall behind them is brighter than their faces. Ask the AV team for more front light on speakers and to lower the screen brightness during speeches.

### What colour of stage light is best for photos?
White light of one colour temperature on faces during speeches and awards. Coloured washes look good on the room but make skin tones look strange, so keep them for walk-ins and performances.

### Where should the logo go on an event backdrop?
At head height and above, repeated across the width, so it appears behind people's heads in every crop instead of being hidden by bodies. Use a matte material to avoid reflections.

### Should the lectern be in front of the screen?
No. Place it to the side of the screen, angled towards the audience, at a height that doesn't hide shorter speakers, with microphones and laptops kept low.

### How can we get better photos at a dimly lit dinner?
Raise the house lights a little during speeches, awards and table photos, light the tables rather than relying on candles, and agree lighting cues with the AV team.
