# Corporate Video Production in Singapore: A Planning Guide

**Short answer:** A corporate or brand video usually takes **4–8 weeks** from brief to final delivery. The work runs in three stages:

1. **Pre-production**: the brief, script, interview plan and shoot schedule
2. **Production**: one or more filming days
3. **Post-production**: editing, music, graphics, subtitles and review rounds

Many corporate video projects we produce fall between **S$10,000 and S$20,000**. Smaller single-day videos cost less, and multi-video or multi-location projects cost more. The biggest factors are the **number of shoot days**, the **number of finished videos** and the **amount of graphics**.

*Written by Ricky Ee, Director of RS Photography. RS Photography produces corporate videos, brand films and campaign videos for companies, government agencies and non-profits in Singapore.*

## Common types of corporate video

| Type | Typical length | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Company profile / brand film** | 2–3 minutes | Website, pitches, events, recruitment |
| **Campaign or launch video** | 60–90 seconds plus short cutdowns | Social media, launch events, advertising |
| **Recruitment / employer brand video** | 1–2 minutes | Careers page, LinkedIn, career fairs |
| **Testimonial / case study video** | 1–2 minutes each | Sales, website, proposals |
| **Explainer or programme video** | 1–3 minutes | Explaining a service, scheme or process |
| **Anniversary or tribute video** | 3–6 minutes | Anniversary events, internal recognition |
| **Training or internal video** | Varies | Onboarding, safety, internal comms |

Decide the **main use** first. A video for a 3-second scroll on LinkedIn is made very differently from one played on a ballroom screen.

## The process, step by step

### 1. Brief (week 1)
Agree on:
- who the video is for
- what it should make viewers think or do
- where it will be shown
- the length
- the deadline
- the key messages, no more than three

Share brand guidelines and any existing videos you like or dislike.

### 2. Concept and script (weeks 1–2)
The production team proposes a concept, an outline and a script or interview plan. For interview-led videos, the "script" is a list of questions and the story the answers should tell.

### 3. Planning (weeks 2–3)
- **Shoot schedule**: locations, times and who is needed where
- **Interviewees**: confirmed, briefed and given the questions in advance
- **Locations**: access and permissions booked
- **Shot list**: interviews, b-roll (supporting footage), and any staged scenes

### 4. Filming (weeks 3–4)
Typical shoot days cover interviews in the morning, when people are fresh, and b-roll of people at work, facilities and activities in the afternoon. A multi-camera interview setup gives editors more choice and a more polished result.

### 5. Editing and review (weeks 4–7)
- **First cut**: structure and story
- **Review round 1**: your feedback on content and order
- **Second cut**: refined edit with music, graphics and subtitles
- **Review round 2**: final changes
- **Final delivery**

Agree on the **number of review rounds** in the quote, usually two or three. Unlimited rounds are the most common reason projects run late.

### 6. Versions and delivery (week 8)
Deliver the main video plus the versions you need:
- a short cutdown, for example 30 or 60 seconds
- vertical versions for social media
- a version without subtitles or with different languages
- a version for screening at events

## Interviews: getting people to look and sound natural

- **Send questions in advance,** but ask interviewees not to memorise answers.
- **Book a quiet room** away from air-conditioning noise, printers and corridors.
- **Allow 30–45 minutes per interviewee,** including setup and a warm-up chat.
- **Ask open questions** ("Tell me about a time when...") rather than yes-or-no questions.
- **Have someone the interviewee trusts** in the room, such as a colleague from their team or comms.

## Locations and permissions

- **Your own premises**: check with building management about access, parking and filming in common areas.
- **Client or partner sites, hospitals and schools**: get written permission, and agree on what may not be filmed, such as patients, students without consent, screens and documents.
- **Public places**: some locations, such as transport stations, parks and malls, have their own rules for filming. Check before you plan scenes there.
- **People in the background**: inform staff and visitors, use consent forms for anyone featured, and avoid identifiable people who have not agreed. See our [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/pdpa-event-photography-consent-guide "PDPA consent guide for photography and video"].

## Music, graphics and subtitles

- **Music**: use properly licensed tracks. Popular songs cannot be used in corporate videos without a licence, and online platforms may mute or remove videos that use them.
- **Graphics**: titles, name captions and simple animations, built from your brand guidelines.
- **Subtitles**: most corporate videos are watched with the sound off on social media, so burned-in subtitles are worth including. Add other languages if your audience needs them.

## What drives the budget

Many corporate video projects we produce fall between **S$10,000 and S$20,000**. The main factors are:

- **shoot days**: one day or several, and how many locations
- **number of finished videos**: one main video, or a series
- **crew and cameras**: single-camera or multi-camera interviews
- **graphics and animation**: simple titles, or custom motion graphics
- **talent**: staff only, or actors and presenters
- **languages and versions**: subtitles, translations and cutdowns
- **review rounds**

## FAQ

### How long does corporate video production take in Singapore?
Usually 4 to 8 weeks from brief to final delivery: about two to three weeks of planning, one or more filming days, and three to four weeks of editing with review rounds.

### How much does a corporate video cost in Singapore?
Many corporate video projects fall between S$10,000 and S$20,000. Simple single-day videos cost less. Projects with several shoot days, several finished videos, custom animation or actors cost more.

### How many review rounds should a corporate video include?
Usually two or three: a first cut for structure and story, a second cut with music, graphics and subtitles, and a final round for small changes. Agree on this in the quotation.

### Do we need a script for an interview-based corporate video?
You need an interview plan instead of a word-for-word script: the questions, who answers them and the story the answers should tell. Send questions to interviewees in advance, but ask them not to memorise answers.

### What versions of the video should we ask for?
Usually the main video, a 30 or 60 second cutdown, vertical versions for social media, and a subtitled version. Add versions in other languages or for event screening if needed.
