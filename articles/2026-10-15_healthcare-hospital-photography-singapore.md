# Hospital and Healthcare Photography in Singapore: A Planning Guide

**Short answer:** Photography in hospitals, polyclinics and other healthcare settings needs everything a corporate shoot needs, plus three things:

- **Patient privacy first.** No patient appears in a photo or video without written consent, and screens, records and wristbands stay out of frame.
- **Clinical areas only with permission and an escort.** Follow the institution's infection-control rules.
- **A clear approval process** with corporate communications before anything is published.

For healthcare events such as conferences, staff awards and anniversaries, the main extra care is keeping patients and clinical information out of the photos.

## Patient privacy and consent

This is the most important part of any healthcare shoot.

- **Written consent for every identifiable patient.** Use the institution's own consent form where one exists. Explain clearly where the photo or video will be used.
- **When in doubt, leave them out.** Avoid filming in waiting areas, wards or corridors where patients could be identified without consent.
- **Watch the background.** Screens, patient records, whiteboards, wristbands, bed labels and medication charts must not be readable in any frame.
- **Patient stories need extra care.** Give patients time to decide, let them withdraw, and agree how much medical detail is shown.
- **Children and vulnerable patients** need consent from parents or guardians, and the institution's own safeguarding rules apply.

Personal data rules also apply to photos of staff and visitors. See our [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/pdpa-event-photography-consent-guide "PDPA consent guide for photography and video"].

## Clinical areas and infection control

- **Permission first.** Clinical areas such as wards, operating theatres, ICUs and labs are only filmed with approval from the department and corporate communications.
- **Always with an escort.** A staff member stays with the crew at all times in clinical areas.
- **Follow infection-control rules.** This may mean hand hygiene, masks, gowns or shoe covers. It may also mean equipment is wiped down before entering certain areas.
- **Keep equipment compact.** Fewer stands and cables means less disruption and less risk in busy wards.
- **Patient care comes first.** If a clinical situation develops, filming stops and the crew steps aside.
- **Staged scenes.** Many "clinical" shots for campaigns are staged with staff playing patients, in an empty ward or a simulation centre, which avoids most privacy issues.

## Healthcare events

Medical conferences, staff awards and anniversaries are photographed much like other corporate events, with a few differences:

- **Keep patients out of event photos,** especially at events held inside hospitals.
- **Staff award ceremonies** often have many recipients from different departments. Name every recipient correctly. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/award-ceremony-photography-singapore "award ceremony photography"].
- **Medical conferences** may include sponsor exhibitions, scientific posters and workshops. Check the organiser's rules on photographing sponsor booths and slides with unpublished data.
- **Ministerial and VIP visits** follow protocol, often with a tour of a ward or new facility. Plan the route and the photo points with corporate communications.

## Staff features and portraits

Doctors, nurses and allied health staff are busy and often on shift. To make shoots easy:

- **Schedule short slots** around shifts, often 10–15 minutes per person.
- **Shoot near their workplace** so they don't have to leave the ward for long.
- **Use uniforms and lanyards correctly,** following the institution's guidelines, and check name badges are current.
- **Keep the look consistent** for staff directories and recruitment pages. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/corporate-headshots-singapore "corporate headshots"].

## Approval and publication

Healthcare organisations usually have strict review processes:

- **Corporate communications approves** every photo and video before publication.
- **Clinical review** may be needed for anything showing procedures, equipment or medical claims.
- **Allow time for review rounds** in the project schedule, especially for videos.
- **Store files securely,** and delete unused footage containing patients according to the agreed retention period.

## FAQ

### Can patients appear in hospital photos and videos?
Only with written informed consent, using the institution's consent form where one exists. Patients should understand where the images will be used and be able to withdraw. When in doubt, keep patients out of the frame.

### Can a photographer film inside wards or operating theatres?
Only with approval from the department and corporate communications, with a staff escort at all times, and following the institution's infection-control rules. Many clinical scenes are staged with staff in an empty ward or simulation centre instead.

### What must not appear in healthcare photos?
Unconsented patients, readable patient records, screens, whiteboards, wristbands, bed labels and medication charts. Check the background of every frame before publishing.

### How are healthcare events different from other corporate events?
They are planned the same way, but patients must be kept out of event photos, medical conference rules on sponsor booths and unpublished data must be followed, and VIP visits often include ward or facility tours planned with corporate communications.

### How long does approval take for healthcare videos?
Allow extra time. Corporate communications usually reviews every photo and video, and clinical review may be needed for procedures, equipment or medical claims. Build at least one extra review round into the schedule.
