# Grassroots and Community Centre Event Photography in Singapore

**Short answer:** Grassroots and community centre events in Singapore, such as **National Day celebrations, festive dinners, family days, block parties and health fairs**, are busy, open to the public and full of residents of all ages. Plan coverage around:

- **the guest of honour**: arrival, walkabout, speech and interactions with residents
- **residents enjoying the event**: families, seniors and children at the activities
- **volunteers and partners**: the people who made it happen
- **group photos**: committees, volunteers and partners with the guest of honour
- **fast delivery**: selected photos the same day for social media and newsletters

Tell residents photography is taking place, and take extra care with **children and seniors**.

## The guest of honour

Community events often have a guest of honour, such as a Member of Parliament or a grassroots adviser.

- **Get the programme**: arrival time, walkabout route, speech and any presentations.
- **Arrival and welcome** by the organising committee.
- **Walkabout**: the guest of honour talking with residents, at stalls and with volunteers. These are the most used photos.
- **Speech and presentations**: tokens of appreciation, awards and certificates.
- **Group photos** with the committee, volunteers and partners.

## Residents of all ages

- **Seniors** taking part in activities, talks and exercise sessions
- **Every community**: Singapore's community events bring together residents of different races, faiths and ages. Show that mix.
- **Genuine moments**: laughter, conversation and participation rather than posed shots

## Volunteers and partners

Grassroots events depend on volunteers.

- **Volunteers at work**: setting up, running stalls, guiding residents
- **Partner organisations**: schools, businesses and community groups with their booths
- **Thank-you group photos** at the end

## Consent, children and seniors

Community events are public, but residents still have a right to privacy:

- **Tell residents** through signage and the emcee that photos will be taken and may be shared.
- **Offer an opt-out**, and respect anyone who asks not to be photographed.
- **Children**: use photos of identifiable children publicly only with parents' agreement.
- **Seniors**: photograph with dignity, and ask before close-ups.
- **Health fairs**: never photograph screening results, forms or medical details.

## Outdoor and void-deck events

Many community events are outdoors, in hard courts, open fields or void decks.

- **Plan for heat and rain**: a covered backup and a decision time.
- **Void decks**: mixed light and pillars; choose positions early.
- **Evening events**: plan lighting for the stage and the crowd.

## FAQ

### What should be photographed at a community centre event?
The guest of honour's arrival, walkabout and speech, residents of all ages enjoying the activities, volunteers and partners at work, presentations, and group photos with the committee and volunteers.

### How do you photograph a guest of honour at a community event?
Get the programme and walkabout route, photograph the arrival and welcome, follow the walkabout to capture conversations with residents and volunteers, then the speech, presentations and group photos.

### Do we need consent to photograph residents at community events?
Tell residents through signage and the emcee that photos will be taken and shared, offer an opt-out, use photos of identifiable children only with parents' agreement, and never photograph screening results or medical details at health fairs.

### How long does a community event shoot take?
Usually two to four hours, or a half day for larger celebrations with performances and a dinner.

### How quickly are community event photos delivered?
Selected photos of the guest of honour and the best moments can be delivered the same day for social media, with the full gallery in folders within a few working days.
