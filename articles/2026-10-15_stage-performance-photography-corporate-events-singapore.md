# Photographing Stage Performances at Corporate Events in Singapore

**Short answer:** Dancers, bands, singers, choirs, magicians and staff performances are often the liveliest part of a corporate dinner or event. To photograph them well:

- **Check the performer's agreement**: some artists restrict photography or video of their act.
- **Watch the rehearsal or sound check**, so the photographer knows the key moments.
- **Use the stage light**, not flash, during performances, unless the performer agrees.
- **Shoot from several positions**: front of stage, the side and a wide view from the back.
- **Capture the audience**: reactions often make the best photos.

## Types of performance

| Performance | What to capture |
|---|---|
| **Dance groups** | Peak moments of movement, formations, the finale |
| **Bands and singers** | The lead singer, each musician, the crowd singing along |
| **Choirs and staff performances** | The whole group, individual faces, colleagues cheering |
| **Magicians and interactive acts** | The reveal, volunteers from the audience, reactions |
| **Cultural performances** | Costumes, key movements, respect for traditional elements |
| **Celebrity or headline acts** | Only as agreed in the artist's contract |

---

## Performer agreements

Professional performers and celebrities often have **contracts that limit photography and recording**:

- **Ask the organiser or agency** what the performer's agreement says about photos and video.
- **Common limits**: photos only during the first few songs, no video, no flash, or approval of photos before publishing.
- **Usage**: photos of a headline act may be allowed for internal use but not for advertising.
- **Staff performances** usually have no such limits, but staff should know photos will be shared.

## Rehearsals and timing

- **Get the running order**: which acts, when, and for how long.
- **Watch the rehearsal or sound check** to learn the key moments: lifts, jumps, solos, the finale.
- **Ask the performers** if there is a moment they especially want photographed.
- **Plan positions** before the show, so the photographer isn't moving across the room during the act.

---

## Stage light and flash

- **Use the stage light**: performance lighting is designed for the show and looks better than flash.
- **No flash during performances** unless agreed; it distracts performers and spoils the atmosphere.
- **Fast lenses and careful settings** handle low and changing light.
- **Coloured lights and haze** look dramatic but can turn faces strange colours. Expect some variation, and ask the AV team for a brighter wash during key moments.

## Positions

- **Front of stage, low and to the side**: close-ups of faces and expressions, without blocking the audience's view.
- **Side of stage**: performers with the audience behind them.
- **Back of the room, raised**: the whole stage, the lighting and the full audience.
- **Never block the view** of guests or the main video camera.

## FAQ

### Can performers at corporate events be photographed?
Usually yes, but check the performer's agreement first. Professional performers and celebrities may limit photos to certain songs, ban video or flash, or require approval before publishing.

### Should a photographer use flash during a stage performance?
Usually not. Stage lighting is designed for the show and looks better, and flash distracts performers. Use fast lenses and careful settings, and use flash only if the performer agrees.

### Where should the photographer stand during a performance?
Low and to the side at the front of the stage for close-ups, at the side of the stage for performers with the audience, and raised at the back for the whole stage, without blocking guests or the video camera.

### How do you capture the key moments of a dance or band performance?
Watch the rehearsal or sound check, get the running order, ask performers which moments matter most, and plan positions before the show.

### Should the audience be photographed during performances?
Yes. Laughter, cheering, singing along and VIPs enjoying the show are often the best photos of the night.
