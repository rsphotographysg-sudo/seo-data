# Same-Day Event Photo Delivery in Singapore: How It Works

**Short answer:** Same-day delivery means an **on-site editor** works next to the photographers. They transfer, select, edit and upload photos while the event is still running, so the first selects arrive **within the hour** and a fuller set by the end of the day.

Standard delivery is a **preview set within 24–48 hours** and the **full edited gallery within 3–5 working days**. Same-day delivery costs more because it adds a person, equipment and a reliable internet connection on site. It is worth it when photos must go out during or right after the event: press releases, LinkedIn posts, sponsor posts or a closing-session slideshow.

## Event photo delivery options at a glance

| Option | What you get | When it arrives | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Live selects | 10–30 edited photos of key moments | Within the hour, during the event | Social posts and press while the event runs |
| Same-day set | 50–150 edited highlights | By the end of the event day | Press releases, internal news, closing slideshow |
| Preview set | A selection of the best images | Within 24–48 hours | Thank-you emails and post-event posts |
| Full gallery | All usable photos, fully edited | Within 3–5 working days | Archives, annual reports, sponsors, speakers |

Most events need only the preview and full gallery. Same-day delivery is an add-on for events where timing matters.

## How same-day delivery works, step by step

Same-day delivery is a workflow, not a faster photographer. A typical setup:

1. **Shoot.** Photographers cover the programme as normal. They shoot the moments on the "must-have now" list first: the guest of honour's arrival, the opening speech, the launch moment.
2. **Transfer.** Photos reach the editor's laptop in one of two ways. The first is a **memory card swap** at agreed times, for example after the opening ceremony. The second is a **wireless transfer** from the camera while shooting. Card swaps are the most reliable in a crowded ballroom where Wi-Fi is congested.
3. **Select.** The editor picks the best frames and removes:
   - closed eyes and blurred shots
   - near-duplicates
   - photos of guests wearing an opt-out lanyard
   - anything showing restricted screens or documents
4. **Edit.** Each photo gets exposure, colour, crop and straightening, using a consistent style set up before the event. Same-day editing is clean and accurate, but it is not the detailed retouching you get in the full gallery.
5. **Upload.** Edited photos go to a private online gallery or shared folder over the venue's network or a dedicated 4G/5G hotspot.
6. **Share.** The organiser's contact gets a link, often with photos named by session, so the comms team can post right away.

The same photos are then fully edited again for the final gallery.

## When you need same-day photos

Same-day delivery is worth the extra cost when the photos have to be used before the event ends, or right after:

- **Press releases and media statements.** Government agencies and listed companies often issue a release the same afternoon.
- **Social media during the event.** LinkedIn and Instagram posts get the most reach while delegates are still in the room and posting themselves.
- **Sponsor commitments.** Some sponsorship packages promise posts on the day.
- **Closing-session slideshows.** A "today in photos" loop before the closing address is a strong ending.
- **Multi-day conferences.** Each evening's set feeds the next morning's recap email and opening slides.
- **Internal comms.** CEO town halls and staff events, where employees expect to see photos on the intranet the same day.

If none of these apply, standard delivery gives you the same photos for less money.

## What affects the cost

Same-day delivery adds three things to a quote:

- **An on-site editor.** A dedicated person for the full event, separate from the photographers.
- **Equipment.** A laptop, card readers, backup drives and a mobile hotspot.
- **Planning time.** Setting up the editing style and the gallery before the event.

The cost depends mostly on the hours the editor is on site and how many photos you need, and how often. A few highlights at lunch and at the close costs less than continuous uploads all day. ## What organisers should prepare

Same-day delivery only works if the organiser side is ready too.

- [ ] **A must-have-now list.** Which 5–10 moments do you need first? Put them in priority order.
- [ ] **A desk and power** for the editor, close to the event but out of guests' way. A spot near the AV control desk works well.
- [ ] **Internet.** A reliable venue connection or permission for the crew's hotspot. Hotel ballroom Wi-Fi often slows down once 500 delegates connect.
- [ ] **One approver.** The person who receives the link and decides what gets posted. Comms teams move faster when they know who signs off.
- [ ] **Captions and names.** Speaker and VIP names with titles, so posts and press photos are captioned correctly.
- [ ] **PDPA arrangements.** Opt-out markers briefed to the whole crew, including the editor. See our [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/pdpa-event-photography-consent-guide "PDPA event photography consent guide"].
- [ ] **Restricted content.** Tell the crew about any screens, documents or people that must not appear in published photos. This matters at government events; see [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/government-event-photography-singapore "government event photography requirements"].

## Same-day quality vs full-gallery quality

Same-day photos are **ready to publish but not final**:

- **Edit.** Same-day photos get exposure, colour and crop. The full gallery adds detailed retouching where needed, such as skin, distracting background objects and stray reflections.
- **Selection.** Same-day photos are the strongest frames of priority moments. The full gallery covers the whole event.
- **File size.** Same-day photos are often web-sized for fast upload. The full gallery is full resolution for print and archives.

Ask your photographer which version to use where. Same-day images are fine for social media and press. For an annual report cover, use the full-gallery version.

## Common problems and how to avoid them

1. **No internet.** Test the connection at the venue walk-through, and have a hotspot as backup.
2. **No one to receive the photos.** The link arrives and nobody posts for three hours. Name an approver in advance.
3. **Too many photos requested too often.** Continuous uploads take time away from selecting and editing well. Agree on batches, for example after the opening, at lunch and at the close.
4. **Opt-out guests in published photos.** Brief the editor as well as the photographers, and select carefully.
5. **Programme changes.** If the guest of honour arrives early, tell the crew at once, or the most important photo may be missed.

## FAQ

### How fast can we get event photos in Singapore?
With an on-site editor, first selects can arrive within the hour and a highlights set by the end of the event day. Without one, expect a preview set within 24 to 48 hours and the full edited gallery within 3 to 5 working days.

### How does same-day event photo delivery work?
An editor works on site with the photographers. Photos are transferred by memory card swaps or wirelessly, then selected, edited and uploaded to a private online gallery while the event is still running.

### Is same-day delivery the same quality as the final gallery?
Same-day photos are edited for exposure, colour and crop, and are ready for social media and press. The final gallery adds detailed retouching and full-resolution files for print.

### What do we need to provide for same-day delivery?
A desk and power for the editor, a reliable internet connection or permission to use a hotspot, a list of priority moments, speaker and VIP names for captions, and one person to receive and approve the photos.

### Does same-day delivery cost more?
Yes. It adds an on-site editor, equipment and an internet connection. The cost depends on how long the editor is on site and how many photos you need, and how often.
