# Restaurant Launch and Media Tasting Photography in Singapore

**Short answer:** Restaurant and F&B launches combine **food photography** and **event photography**, and food looks its best for only a few minutes. Plan the day in this order:

1. **Hero dishes first**, photographed before guests arrive, with the chef's plating and good light
2. **The space**: dining room, bar, kitchen and details, while it is empty and clean
3. **The chef and team**: portraits and action in the kitchen
4. **The event**: guests, media and influencers tasting, the chef presenting, the opening moment

Agree with the chef which dishes to photograph and when, so food is plated fresh for the camera.

## Hero dishes: before guests arrive

Food photographed during a busy event rarely looks its best. Plan a **separate session before doors open**:

- **Get the dish list** from the chef, with plating times.
- **One dish at a time**, plated fresh and photographed immediately.
- **Good light**: a table near a window, or soft lighting set up for the session.
- **Several angles**: overhead, 45 degrees and close-up details.
- **Simple props**: the restaurant's own plates, cutlery and table settings.
- **Don't touch the food** without the chef's agreement; let the kitchen adjust plating.

---

## The space

- **Photograph empty and clean**, before guests arrive.
- **Wide shots** of the dining room, bar and any private rooms.
- **Details**: lighting, tableware, artwork, signage and the entrance.
- **Day and night**, if the atmosphere changes.

## The chef and team

- **Chef portrait**: in the dining room or the kitchen, in uniform.
- **Action in the kitchen**: plating, cooking and the pass (only where safe and allowed).
- **Team photo**: kitchen and front-of-house together.

---

## The event: media and guests

- **The chef presenting** each dish to the table
- **Media and guests tasting**, with natural reactions (avoid unflattering photos of people mid-bite)
- **Influencers photographing the food**: these photos show buzz
- **The opening moment**: ribbon-cutting, a toast or a speech
- **Partners and owners** with key guests

### Low restaurant light
Restaurants are often dim. Photographers use fast lenses and discreet flash where appropriate, but **a little more light on the tables** during the event makes a big difference. See [INTERNAL LINK: /insights/event-stage-lighting-backdrop-photos "lighting that photographs well"].

## Privacy

- **Tell guests** about photography, especially at public openings.
- **Respect opt-outs**; some diners at a soft opening may not want to appear.
- **Ask before featuring** any guest prominently in marketing.

## FAQ

### When should food be photographed at a restaurant launch?
Before guests arrive, in a separate session with the chef plating each dish fresh. Food photographed during a busy event rarely looks its best.

### What should be photographed at a media tasting?
Each dish as it is served, the chef presenting and explaining, media and guests tasting with natural reactions, influencers photographing the food, and the opening moment.

### How do you photograph food in a dim restaurant?
Photograph hero dishes before the event near a window or with soft lighting, then use fast lenses and discreet flash during the event. Adding a little light on the tables helps.

### What should a restaurant media kit include?
Captioned photos of hero dishes, chef and team portraits, and photos of the space, delivered in a folder journalists can download quickly.

### How long does restaurant launch photography take?
Usually a half day: a dish and space session before doors open, followed by the event. Large menus or separate drinks sessions add time.
