# Groundbreaking and Opening Ceremony Photography in Singapore

**Short answer:** Groundbreaking, topping-out and official opening ceremonies are short events built around **one key moment**:

- the shovels turning the soil
- the ribbon being cut
- the plaque being unveiled

Plan that moment carefully:

- a **clear central camera position**
- **all VIPs facing the camera**
- a **pause after the action**
- **two angles** if possible

On construction sites, the crew also needs to follow **site safety rules**, including PPE and safety briefings.

## Types of ceremony

| Ceremony | Key moment | Typical setting |
|---|---|---|
| **Groundbreaking** | VIPs turning soil with ceremonial shovels, or pressing a button | Construction site, often in a tent |
| **Topping-out** | The final beam or concrete pour, or a symbolic signing | Construction site, sometimes at height |

## Getting the key moment right

The key moment usually lasts only seconds, and it appears in every press release, annual report and plaque photo afterwards.

### Before the moment
- **Rehearse with the emcee and protocol officer,** ideally with the photographer present.
- **Mark VIP positions** on the floor or stage, in order of precedence.
- **Check the background**: the backdrop, logo, building or site, with nothing distracting behind the VIPs.

### During the moment
- **Reserve a central camera position** in line with the VIPs, kept clear of media and guests.
- **Ask everyone to face the camera** while doing the action: shovels in the soil, scissors on the ribbon, hands on the plaque curtain.
- **Hold for a few seconds** after the action. The emcee can cue: "Please hold for the photographers."
- **Shoot two angles**: a central main shot, and a second photographer for a wider or side view.

### After the moment
- **Group photo** of all VIPs at the same spot, then with partners or the project team.
- **Close-ups** of the plaque, ribbon, shovels or signage.

## Construction site safety

Groundbreaking and topping-out ceremonies often happen on live construction sites.

- **Safety briefing.** All crew attend the site safety briefing.
- **PPE.** Crew wear the required personal protective equipment, usually safety helmets, safety footwear and high-visibility vests, as set by the site.
- **Stay in permitted areas.** Photographers move only in areas the site safety officer has approved.
- **Working at height.** For topping-out ceremonies, crew follow the site's rules for working at height and only use approved platforms.
- **Drones** need the right permits, a licensed operator and site approval. Plan this well in advance.

## Guest of honour and protocol

Ceremonies often have a minister, senior officials or a company chairman as guest of honour.

- **Follow the order of precedence** for positions in the key moment and group photos.
- **Plan the route**: arrival, welcome, tour of the site or building, speeches, key moment and departure. The photographer needs the timings.
- **Site or facility tours** are often the best candid shots. Plan where the photographer walks so they stay ahead of the group.
- **Media** may be present. Reserve your photographer's position separately from the media area.

## FAQ

### How do we get a good photo of the ribbon cutting or groundbreaking?
Rehearse with the emcee and protocol officer, mark VIP positions in order of precedence, reserve a central camera position, ask VIPs to face the camera during the action, and hold for a few seconds afterwards. A second photographer adds another angle.

### What safety requirements apply to photographers at a construction site ceremony?
Crew attend the site safety briefing, wear the required PPE such as helmets, safety footwear and high-visibility vests, stay in approved areas, and follow the site's rules for working at height. Many sites require vendors to be bizSAFE certified.

### Can we get ceremony photos on the same day for a press release?
Yes. With an on-site editor, selected photos of the key moment and the VIP group photo can be delivered on the day, captioned with names and titles.

### Should we photograph construction progress before the opening?
It is worth it. Regular photos from fixed points, from groundbreaking to completion, can be used in project reports and in a before-and-after video at the opening ceremony.

### How long does a groundbreaking or opening ceremony shoot take?
Most ceremonies are covered in a half-day booking, including setup, arrivals, speeches, the key moment, group photos and any site or facility tour.
